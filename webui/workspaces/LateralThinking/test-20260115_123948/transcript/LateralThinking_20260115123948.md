### Provocation

> 1. Make plastic invisible. 2. Make plastic 'worthless'. 3. Make disposal 'frictionless'.

### Application

Deliberately looking at how to worsen the problem (making plastic invisible, worthless, and disposal frictionless) to uncover hidden assumptions, then flipping those insights into innovative solutions.

### Generated Ideas (3)

#### 1. The 'Plastic-to-Access' Micro-Economy

**Novelty:** 80.0% | **Feasibility:** 90.0%

The city installs automated 'Reverse Vending Machines' at the entrances to high-traffic public amenities (subways, public pools, museums, or even premium parking zones). Instead of cash, these gates accept a specific weight of clean, recyclable plastic as a 'Green Pass' for entry or a significant discount.

**Breakthrough Aspect:** It shifts plastic from 'trash to be collected' to 'a ticket to be earned.' It turns the citizen from a 'polluter' into a 'prospector' who actively hunts for plastic to subsidize their lifestyle.

**Benefits:**
- Subsidizes citizen lifestyle
- Encourages active cleanup of public spaces
- Reduces municipal collection costs by centralizing drop-off points

**Challenges:**
- Maintenance of reverse vending machines
- Ensuring plastic is clean and correctly sorted by users
- Integration with existing transit and amenity gate systems

---

#### 2. The 'Ghost Infrastructure' (Hyper-Visibility)

**Novelty:** 70.0% | **Feasibility:** 70.0%

Replace all opaque public trash bins with tall, transparent, graduated cylinders equipped with LED sensors. As the bin fills, the light changes from a soft green to a pulsing 'emergency' red. Furthermore, the city uses the collected plastic to 3D-print 'Street Upgrades' (benches, planters, bike racks) on-site in a mobile transparent trailer.

**Breakthrough Aspect:** It uses 'social shame' and 'visual progress' as psychological levers. It stops the 'disappearance' of waste and makes the circular economy a public performance.

**Benefits:**
- Increased public awareness of waste volume
- Immediate tangible results from recycling efforts
- Reduced transport costs by processing waste into products on-site

**Challenges:**
- High initial investment in mobile 3D-printing and shredding technology
- Public space requirements for mobile processing trailers
- Potential for vandalism of transparent bins

---

#### 3. The 'Friction-Incentive' Disposal System

**Novelty:** 85.0% | **Feasibility:** 80.0%

A 'Reverse Lottery Bin' where users must scan a QR code on their phone to deposit plastic. The bin weighs the deposit and instantly enters the user into a 'Municipal Lottery' where the prize is a reduction in their monthly utility bill or property tax. Standard trash bins are made smaller and harder to find.

**Breakthrough Aspect:** It introduces 'positive friction.' It makes the user stop and think, 'Is this plastic worth a lottery ticket?' It flips the 'convenience' model on its head by rewarding the effort of sorting.

**Benefits:**
- Gamification of civic responsibility
- Direct financial incentives for citizens
- Higher quality of sorted recyclables due to the scanning/weighing process

**Challenges:**
- Digital divide issues for citizens without smartphones
- Retrofitting existing bins with smart technology and scales
- Managing the lottery system and utility bill integration

### Provocation

> How can we apply the principles of Jazz, Ant Colonies, and Origami to solve the urban plastic waste crisis?

### Application

Using the Random Stimulus technique to force connections between unrelated concepts (Jazz, Ant Colonies, and Origami) and urban plastic waste to uncover non-obvious solutions and break traditional management patterns.

### Generated Ideas (3)

#### 1. The 'Syncopated Bin' Network

**Novelty:** 75.0% | **Feasibility:** 85.0%

Implement 'Musical Micro-Hubs' across the city. These are smart bins equipped with low-cost acoustic sensors. When a citizen deposits plastic, the bin generates a specific musical note or 'riff.' If multiple people deposit plastic within a certain timeframe, the bins sync via IoT to create a collaborative 'neighborhood melody.'

**Breakthrough Aspect:** The stimulus shifted the focus from disposal (an end-point) to performance (an ongoing engagement).

**Benefits:**
- Turns a chore into a creative, social performance
- Encourages citizen participation through gamification
- High-traffic areas can unlock complex compositions as recycling targets are met

**Challenges:**
- Maintenance of electronic sensors in outdoor environments
- Potential noise pollution concerns in residential areas
- Ensuring reliable IoT connectivity across the bin network

---

#### 2. Digital Pheromone Foraging

**Novelty:** 80.0% | **Feasibility:** 70.0%

A 'Digital Pheromone' App where citizens 'drop a pheromone' (a geo-tagged alert) on a city map when they see litter. This increases the 'scent' of that location. Other citizens acting as 'foragers' can see these high-scent areas, collect the plastic, and bring it to a local 'mound' (processing hub) to earn micro-credits or 'larvae points.'

**Breakthrough Aspect:** The stimulus moved the solution from centralized logistics to decentralized collective intelligence.

**Benefits:**
- Gamifies clean-up as a collective survival strategy
- Reduces the need for additional municipal trucks
- Leverages existing citizen movement and walking routes

**Challenges:**
- Requires a critical mass of users to be effective
- Need for a robust system to verify plastic collection
- Establishing a network of local processing hubs ('mounds')

---

#### 3. The 'Fold-to-Function' Modular Brick

**Novelty:** 90.0% | **Feasibility:** 65.0%

The city distributes low-cost, manual 'Origami Presses' to households. Citizens shred their own plastic waste and press it into standardized, interlocking 'Urban Bricks.' These bricks are then collected by the city to be used directly in local infrastructure projects like community garden beds or park benches.

**Breakthrough Aspect:** The stimulus changed the perception of plastic from 'volume to be removed' to 'geometry to be repurposed.'

**Benefits:**
- Citizens become manufacturers for their own neighborhood
- Reduces the volume of waste for transport by 90% at the source
- Drastically cuts municipal fuel and logistics costs

**Challenges:**
- Initial investment required for home-press distribution
- Establishing and maintaining safety standards for the produced bricks
- Ensuring consistent quality of bricks from different households
