## Top Breakthrough Ideas
1. **The 'Civic Equity' Currency** (Exaggeration (Value))
   The Plastic-for-Permits Program. Citizens use a mobile app to scan and deposit plastic at smart kiosks. Instead of pennies, they earn 'Civic Credits' that can be used to pay for parking tickets, library late fees, public pool entries, or even property tax micro-rebates.

2. **Pheromone Pathing (Decentralized Foraging Network)** (Random Stimulus)
   A digital app allows citizens to 'drop a scent' (geo-tag) when they see plastic litter or full bins. As more people tag an area, the 'scent' grows stronger on a public heat map. Local 'Foragers' (gig workers, students, or retirees) are incentivized with micro-payments or 'City Credits' to clear these high-scent areas.

3. **The Plastic-to-Transit Tokenization** (Challenging Assumptions)
   A blockchain-based 'Plastic Credit' system where every piece of plastic has a micro-value. Citizens sell sorted plastic to 'Micro-Transporters' (gig workers) who use bike trailers to move plastic to hubs, earning credits for transit or utilities.

4. **"The Pavement Harvest" (In-Situ Integration)** (Reversal Technique)
   The city replaces traditional asphalt repairs with a "Modular Plastic Paving" system. Citizens are provided with simple, manual "hand-presses" (distributed at community centers). They collect their own plastic, shred it, and press it into standardized, interlocking paving studs used to repair potholes or create bike lanes in their own street.

5. **The "Ghost of Consumption" (Hyper-Visibility)** (Reversal Technique)
   The city uses Augmented Reality (AR) and "Smart Bins" to project a digital "trash cloud" over neighborhoods or individual households. The more plastic discarded, the larger and more intrusive the digital cloud becomes on the city’s official app.

## Recommended Approaches
- **In-Situ Infrastructure Integration:** Stop "collecting" plastic. Instead, provide citizens with the tools (small-scale melters/presses) to turn their own waste into "Modular Paving" blocks to repair their own sidewalks and potholes.
- **The "Invisible Bin" Strategy:** Drastically reduce the number of public bins to increase the "social friction" of disposal, while simultaneously launching a "Material Leasing" app that rewards users for returning plastic to specific high-value "Resource Hubs."
- **Volumetric Incentives:** Shift from weight-based recycling to volume-based rewards. Use "Origami-ready" designs to teach the public that the "Logistics of Air" is the enemy, rewarding those who minimize the spatial footprint of their waste.
- **Blockchain Material Tracking:** Treat every piece of plastic as a "Civic Asset" with a micro-bounty. This turns every citizen into a potential "forager," making it economically impossible for plastic to remain on the ground for long.

## Feasibility Assessment
The proposed ideas represent a shift from centralized industrial processing to decentralized behavioral and micro-manufacturing models. High feasibility is found in digital infrastructure and gig-economy models (Pheromone Pathing, Civic Equity), while medium feasibility applies to physical infrastructure (Invisible Bin, Plastic-to-Transit). Low feasibility/high risk is associated with distributed industrial machinery (Pavement Harvest, Print-Farm) due to material purity and maintenance challenges.
