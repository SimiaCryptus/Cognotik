## Game Theory Analysis

**Scenario:** Two suspects are arrested for a crime. The police have insufficient evidence for a conviction on the principal charge, but enough to convict both on a lesser charge. 
The police offer each prisoner a bargain:
- If A and B both betray each other, each of them serves 5 years in prison.
- If A betrays B but B remains silent, A is set free and B serves 10 years in prison.
- If A remains silent but B betrays A, A serves 10 years in prison and B is set free.
- If A and B both remain silent, both of them serve 1 year in prison.
**Players:** Suspect A, Suspect B

**Game Type:** non-cooperative

## Game Structure Analysis
<details><summary>Full Analysis</summary>

This scenario represents the classic **Prisoner’s Dilemma**, a fundamental model in game theory that demonstrates why two completely rational individuals might not cooperate, even if it appears in their best interests to do so.

### 1. Identify the Game Structure
*   **Game Type**: 
    *   **Non-cooperative**: The players make decisions independently, and there is no enforceable agreement or contract between them.
    *   **Non-zero-sum**: The total "utility" (or cost) is not constant. The total years served by both players varies from 2 years (1+1) to 10 years (5+5 or 0+10).
    *   **Simultaneous**: Both players make their decisions without knowing the choice of the other. Even if the physical timing differs, the information set is simultaneous because neither has observed the other's move.
*   **One-shot vs. Repeated**: This is a **one-shot game**. There is no future interaction mentioned, meaning players do not need to worry about retaliation or reputation in subsequent rounds.
*   **Information**: 
    *   **Imperfect Information**: Players do not know the action chosen by the other player at the time of their decision.
    *   **Complete Information**: Both players have full knowledge of the payoff matrix, the strategies available, and the motivations of the other player.
*   **Symmetry**: This is a **symmetric game**. The payoffs for playing a particular strategy depend only on the other strategies employed, not on which player is playing them. If the players swapped identities, the payoffs would remain the same.

### 2. Define Strategy Spaces
*   **Available Strategies**: 
    *   $S_A = \{Betray, Silent\}$
    *   $S_B = \{Betray, Silent\}$
*   **Nature of Strategies**: The strategies are **discrete** (binary). There are no intermediate options (e.g., "partially betray").
*   **Constraints**: The primary constraint is the **lack of communication**. The suspects are isolated, preventing them from negotiating or making binding promises.

### 3. Characterize Payoffs
*   **Objectives**: Each player’s objective is to **minimize their own prison sentence** (maximize individual utility). In game theory terms, we represent these as negative values (disutility).
*   **Payoff Matrix (A, B)**:
    | | Suspect B: Silent | Suspect B: Betray |
    | :--- | :---: | :---: |
    | **Suspect A: Silent** | (-1, -1) | (-10, 0) |
    | **Suspect A: Betray** | (0, -10) | (-5, -5) |
*   **Transferability**: Payoffs are **non-transferable**. One suspect cannot "give" years of their sentence to the other, nor can they side-pay each other to influence decisions within the context of the game.

### 4. Identify Key Features
*   **Dominant Strategy**: For both players, **"Betray" is a strictly dominant strategy**. 
    *   If B is silent, A is better off betraying (0 years) than being silent (1 year).
    *   If B betrays, A is better off betraying (5 years) than being silent (10 years).
    *   The same logic applies to Suspect B.
*   **Nash Equilibrium**: The unique Nash Equilibrium is **(Betray, Betray)**. Neither player can improve their outcome by unilaterally changing their strategy, even though this leads to a worse collective outcome.
*   **Pareto Efficiency**: The Nash Equilibrium is **Pareto inefficient**. There exists another outcome—(Silent, Silent)—where *both* players would be better off (1 year each vs. 5 years each). However, this outcome is unstable because both players have an individual incentive to cheat (betray) to reach 0 years.
*   **Communication and Signaling**: There are no opportunities for signaling or commitment. Even if the players could shout through the walls and agree to stay silent, the "cheap talk" would not change the underlying incentive to betray once the police enter the room, as the agreement is not enforceable.
*   **Timing**: The moves are effectively simultaneous. The police have structured the timing to ensure that the "shadow of the future" does not influence the current decision.

### Summary Analysis
The game highlights the conflict between **individual rationality** and **collective rationality**. While the group would benefit most from mutual silence (2 total years), the individual incentive to avoid the "sucker's payoff" (10 years) and chase the "temptation payoff" (0 years) drives both players to a sub-optimal equilibrium of mutual betrayal (10 total years).

This scenario is the classic **Prisoner's Dilemma**, a fundamental model in game theory that demonstrates why two completely rational individuals might not cooperate, even if it appears in their best interest to do so.

### 1. Game Structure Analysis

*   **Game Type**: 
    *   **Non-cooperative**: Players make decisions independently without the ability to form binding contracts.
    *   **Simultaneous**: Both players choose their strategy at the same time without knowing the other's choice.
    *   **One-shot**: The interaction occurs only once, meaning there is no opportunity to build trust or retaliate in future rounds.
    *   **Non-zero-sum**: The total "utility" or years served is not constant; the collective outcome varies based on the choices made (ranging from 2 total years to 10 total years).
*   **Information**: 
    *   **Imperfect Information**: Neither player knows the other's move until after the decision is made.
    *   **Complete Information**: Both players are fully aware of the rules, the strategies available, and the resulting payoffs for both parties.
*   **Strategy Spaces**: 
    *   **Discrete**: Each player has exactly two choices: **Betray** (Defect) or **Silent** (Cooperate).
*   **Symmetry**: 
    *   **Symmetric**: The payoffs for a strategy pair depend only on the strategies themselves, not on which player is playing them. If the players swapped roles, the payoffs would remain identical.

### 3. Detailed Outcome Analysis

#### Outcome: (Silent, Silent)
*   **Outcome for Players**: Both serve 1 year.
*   **Numerical Payoff**: (-1, -1)
*   **Explanation**: This is the **Socially Optimal** outcome. By cooperating with each other (remaining silent), they minimize their total collective time in prison (2 years total). However, this state is unstable because each player has an individual incentive to cheat.

#### Outcome: (Betray, Silent)
*   **Outcome for Players**: Suspect A is set free; Suspect B serves 10 years.
*   **Numerical Payoff**: (0, -10)
*   **Explanation**: Suspect A "free-rides" on Suspect B’s cooperation. A receives the highest possible individual payoff (0 years) by betraying B, while B receives the "Sucker’s Payoff" (the worst possible outcome).

#### Outcome: (Silent, Betray)
*   **Outcome for Players**: Suspect A serves 10 years; Suspect B is set free.
*   **Numerical Payoff**: (-10, 0)
*   **Explanation**: The inverse of the previous scenario. Suspect B acts rationally in their own self-interest to avoid prison, leaving Suspect A to face the maximum penalty for remaining loyal.

#### Outcome: (Betray, Betray)
*   **Outcome for Players**: Both serve 5 years.
*   **Numerical Payoff**: (-5, -5)
*   **Explanation**: This is the **Nash Equilibrium**. 
    *   If A thinks B will stay silent, A should betray (0 is better than -1). 
    *   If A thinks B will betray, A should also betray (-5 is better than -10). 
    *   Since "Betray" is the **Dominant Strategy** for both players, they both choose it. Even though (-1, -1) is better for both, they end up at (-5, -5) because neither can trust the other to remain silent in a non-cooperative, one-shot environment.
