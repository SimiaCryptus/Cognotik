## Game Economy Design

**Game:** Galactic Trader

**Type:** strategy

**Progression Style:** linear

## Resource System Design

In **Galactic Trader**, despite the title, the "trading" occurs internally through a complex resource conversion and logistics system rather than an external marketplace. The player acts as a mobile industrial platform, converting the raw chaos of space into structured progression.

---

### 1. Resource Identity: **Raw Ore**
*   **Type**: Material (Primary)
*   **Purpose**: The foundational building block for all physical construction and the primary input for the crafting system.
*   **Thematic Fit**: Represents the bulk materials (iron, silicates, ice) harvested from asteroid belts. It grounds the player in the "industrial" fantasy of space strategy.

### 2. Resource Identity: **Plasma Cells**
*   **Type**: Energy (Operational)
*   **Purpose**: Acts as the "action point" equivalent. It powers the refinery, fuels long-range jumps between sectors, and maintains ship life support.
*   **Thematic Fit**: Represents the volatile fuel needed to keep a massive ship operational in the vacuum of space.

### 3. Resource Identity: **Refined Composites**
*   **Type**: Crafted Material (Advanced)
*   **Purpose**: The primary gate for ship upgrades and progression. It cannot be found; it must be manufactured.
*   **Thematic Fit**: Represents high-tech alloys and carbon-nanotubes created by processing Raw Ore with high energy.

### 4. Resource Identity: **Navigation Data**
*   **Type**: Experience/Progression (Meta)
*   **Purpose**: Used to unlock new sectors of the galaxy and permanent "Tech Tree" blueprints. It represents the player's linear journey through the stars.
*   **Thematic Fit**: Represents the knowledge gained from scanning anomalies and successfully navigating dangerous sectors.

---

### Resource Generation & Consumption

 Resource | Generation Source | Frequency | Consumption Use | Value Prop |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Raw Ore** | Mining Asteroids (Active click or Drone-based) | High (Constant) | Crafting Refined Composites; Basic Hull Repairs | Essential for all growth; the "grind" element. |
 **Plasma Cells** | Solar Scoops (Passive over time); Gas Giant Siphoning | Medium (Tick-based) | Powering the Refinery; Jumping to new sectors | Limits session length/pacing; forces strategic movement. |
 **Refined Composites** | **Crafting**: 100 Ore + 10 Plasma = 1 Composite | Low (Batch-based) | Ship Module Upgrades (Engine, Cargo, Mining Laser) | Permanent power spikes; allows access to harder zones. |
 **Navigation Data** | Scanning Anomalies; Reaching Sector Milestones | Rare (Milestone-based) | Unlocking new Sectors; Tech Tree nodes | The "Key" to the next level of the linear progression. |

---

### Storage and Limits

*   **Cargo Bay (Raw Ore & Composites)**:
    *   **Limit**: Fixed capacity (e.g., 1,000 units).
    *   **Rationale**: Prevents infinite hoarding and forces the player to choose between keeping raw materials for repairs or refined materials for upgrades.
    *   **Overflow**: Excess ore is vented into space (lost) unless the player upgrades their Cargo Bay.
*   **Battery Array (Plasma Cells)**:
    *   **Limit**: Capped by ship level.
    *   **Rationale**: Prevents players from "stockpiling" energy to skip through multiple sectors at once.
*   **Data Bank (Navigation Data)**:
    *   **Limit**: Infinite.
    *   **Rationale**: As a progression metric, losing "experience" feels punitive in a linear strategy game.

---

### Exchange Rates (Internal Conversion)

Since external trading is disabled, the economy relies on **Conversion Ratios**:

1.  **The Refining Loop**: 10x **Raw Ore** + 1x **Plasma Cell** $\rightarrow$ 1x **Refined Composite**.
2.  **Emergency Conversion**: Players can "Burn" **Refined Composites** to generate **Plasma Cells** (1:5 ratio) if they are stranded in a dark sector without solar energy.
3.  **Data Synthesis**: 50x **Refined Composites** can be "Analyzed" to generate 1x **Navigation Data** (a late-game sink to speed up progression).

---

### Sink Mechanisms & Inflation Prevention

To ensure the economy doesn't stagnate as the player moves through the linear sectors:

*   **Maintenance Tax**: As the ship grows larger (more modules), the **Plasma Cell** consumption per minute increases. This prevents the player from staying in low-level sectors indefinitely, as they will eventually run an energy deficit.
*   **Exponential Crafting Costs**: Linear progression is maintained by increasing the **Refined Composite** requirements for each subsequent ship tier (e.g., Tier 1 costs 10, Tier 2 costs 50, Tier 3 costs 250).
*   **Sector Entry Fees**: Moving to a new sector requires a one-time massive burst of **Plasma Cells** and **Navigation Data**, acting as a "gear check" for the player's current industrial output.
*   **Ablative Armor**: High-level sectors contain "Micrometeoroid Storms" that slowly deplete **Raw Ore** for automatic hull maintenance, ensuring that even basic resources remain relevant in the end-game.

---

### Strategic Choice Example
A player enters a new sector with a full cargo hold. They have enough **Refined Composites** to either:
1.  **Upgrade Mining Lasers**: Increases **Raw Ore** gain speed (Short-term efficiency).
2.  **Upgrade Solar Scoops**: Increases **Plasma Cell** generation (Long-term sustainability).
3.  **Save for Navigation Data**: Pushes them closer to the next Sector (Linear progression).

The lack of trading forces the player to be their own supplier, making every crafting decision a choice between immediate survival and long-term expansion.

## Progression System Design

This design document outlines the economic and progression framework for **Galactic Trader**. Despite the title, the "Trading" aspect is handled through a **Contract Fulfillment System** (Resource -> Reward) rather than a free-market exchange, adhering to the "Trading: False" constraint.

---

# Part 1: Resource System Design

The economy revolves around four pillars: **Scrap**, **Isotopes**, **Credits**, and **Data**.

### 1. Resource Identity
 Resource | Type | Purpose | Thematic Fit |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Scrap Metal** | Material | Basic building block for ship parts and station modules. | Salvaged from derelicts and asteroids. |
 **Ion Isotopes** | Material | High-energy fuel and catalyst for advanced crafting. | Harvested from gas giants and nebulae. |
 **Galactic Credits** | Currency | Used for maintenance, hiring crew, and instant upgrades. | The universal medium of exchange. |
 **Nav-Data** | Experience | Used to level up the player and unlock new sectors. | Compiled sensor logs and star charts. |

### 2. Generation Sources
*   **Scrap Metal:** Acquired via "Salvage Missions." (50–200 per mission). Scales with ship "Extractor" stats.
*   **Ion Isotopes:** Acquired via "Gas Siphoning." (20–80 per mission). Frequency is lower than Scrap but yields are higher in deep space.
*   **Galactic Credits:** Earned by completing **Supply Contracts** (turning in crafted goods). Rewards scale with contract complexity.
*   **Nav-Data:** Generated passively during travel and actively by scanning new anomalies. (10–50 per scan).

### 3. Consumption Uses
*   **Scrap Metal:** Crafting Tier 1–2 components (Hull plates, cargo bays). 100–500 per craft.
*   **Ion Isotopes:** Crafting Tier 3+ components (Warp drives, shields) and refueling long-range jumps.
*   **Galactic Credits:** Ship repairs (10% of current wealth), hiring specialists, and bypassing crafting timers.
*   **Nav-Data:** Spent at "Data Hubs" to increase Player Level. Leveling costs follow the linear progression curve.

### 4. Storage and Limits
*   **Cargo Hold:** Limits Scrap and Isotopes. Can be expanded via crafting.
*   **Digital Wallet:** Credits have no cap to encourage long-term hoarding for end-game "Mega-Structures."
*   **Data Buffer:** Nav-Data is capped per level. Players must "upload" (level up) to continue earning more, preventing over-leveling in starting zones.

### 5. Exchange Rates (Internal Conversion)
*   **Refining:** 5x Scrap + 1x Isotope = 1 **Advanced Alloy** (Crafted Material).
*   **Data Mining:** 1,000 Credits can be spent to buy "Sensor Probes" which generate Nav-Data.
*   *Note: No direct player-to-player trading is enabled.*

### 6. Sink Mechanisms
*   **Maintenance Fees:** Every jump costs a small amount of Credits based on ship size.
*   **Research Decay:** High-level Skill Tree nodes require periodic "Data Maintenance" (small Nav-Data costs) to keep active.
*   **Contract Failure:** Failing a timed contract results in a Credit penalty (insurance bond).

# Part 2: Progression System

The progression uses a **Linear Growth Model**.
*   **Formula:** $XP\_Required = 1000 + (Level \times 1000)$
*   **Level 1:** 1,000 Nav-Data
*   **Level 15:** 15,000 Nav-Data
*   **Level 30:** 30,000 Nav-Data
*   **Total XP to Max:** 465,000 Nav-Data.

*   **Casual (2 hrs/day):** ~60 days.

# Part 3: Skill Trees

Players earn 1 **Skill Point** per level (29 total).

### Branch 1: The Industrialist (Crafting & Efficiency)
*   **S1: Material Recovery:** 10% chance to not consume Scrap when crafting.
*   **S2: Rapid Assembly:** Reduces crafting time by 20%.
*   **S3: Master Smith:** Unlocks "Masterwork" item quality (15% stat boost).
*   **Synergy:** If Branch 2 is also active, "Masterwork" engines use 10% less fuel.

### Branch 2: The Navigator (Exploration & Speed)
*   **S1: Warp Tuning:** Increases travel speed between sectors by 15%.
*   **S2: Sensor Ghost:** Reduces the chance of pirate interceptions by 25%.
*   **S3: Deep Scan:** Increases Nav-Data gain from anomalies by 20%.
*   **Synergy:** If Branch 3 is also active, scanning an anomaly also reveals nearby Scrap deposits.

### Branch 3: The Logistics Expert (Yield & Storage)
*   **S1: Cargo Compression:** Increases storage capacity for Scrap/Isotopes by 30%.
*   **S2: Contract Negotiation:** Increases Credit rewards from contracts by 15%.
*   **S3: Bulk Handling:** Allows fulfilling two contracts simultaneously.
*   **Synergy:** If Branch 1 is also active, fulfilling contracts grants a 5% refund on materials used to craft the items.

### Respec Mechanics
*   **The Neural Reset:** Players can visit a "Med-Bay" in any major hub.
*   **Cost:** Costs 25% of current Galactic Credits. The cost scales with wealth to ensure it remains a meaningful decision but never becomes impossible for a struggling player.

## Loot & Reward System Design

This design builds upon the **Galactic Trader** economy, focusing on a linear progression strategy game where "trading" is the thematic wrapper for resource management and crafting. Since player-to-player trading is disabled, the loot system serves as the primary engine for progression.

---

### 1. Loot Tables by Content Type

The loot system uses a **Weighted Random Distribution**. As the player moves to higher-tier sectors (Linear Progression), the base quantity of resources increases, but the rarity tiers remain governed by these tables.

#### A. Common Enemies / Activities (e.g., Pirate Scouts, Asteroid Mining)
*Focus: Sustaining the economy and providing crafting "filler."*
*   **Star-Iron (Common):** 70% weight (Quantity: 10-20)
*   **Plasma Cells (Uncommon):** 20% weight (Quantity: 2-5)
*   **Basic Component Scrap:** 9% weight (Used for low-tier crafting)
*   **Void Shard (Rare):** 1% weight (Quantity: 1)

#### B. Elite Enemies / Challenges (e.g., Mercenary Captains, Ion Storms)
*Focus: Providing the "bottleneck" materials for mid-tier upgrades.*
*   **Plasma Cells (Uncommon):** 50% weight (Quantity: 15-25)
*   **Void Shards (Rare):** 30% weight (Quantity: 2-4)
*   **Advanced Tech Blueprints (Rare):** 15% weight (One-time unlock)
*   **Galactic Credits (Currency):** 5% weight (High volume)

#### C. Boss Encounters (e.g., Sector Dreadnoughts)
*Focus: Major progression leaps and unique unlocks.*
*   **Void Shards (Rare):** 100% (Guaranteed Quantity: 10-15)
*   **Prototype Core (Epic):** 100% (Guaranteed 1 per boss; required for ship tier-ups)
*   **Legendary Hull Plating:** 10% weight (Cosmetic or high-stat variant)
*   **Massive Credit Bounty:** 100% (Guaranteed)

---

### 2. Rarity and Scaling

| Rarity        | Color  | Scaling Logic         | Role in Crafting                      |
|:--------------|:-------|:----------------------|:--------------------------------------|
| **Common**    | White  | +10% qty per Sector   | Bulk structure and hull repairs.      |
| **Rare**      | Blue   | +5% qty per Sector    | Engine upgrades and weapon systems.   |
| **Epic**      | Purple | Fixed (1-2 per Elite) | Specialized modules (Shields, Cloak). |
| **Legendary** | Gold   | Fixed (Boss only)     | Final-tier ship transformations.      |

**Scaling Mechanic:** To maintain the linear progression, loot quantity is multiplied by the **Sector Tier (T)**. 
*Formula: Base Drop × (1 + (0.5 × (T-1)))*
This ensures that while a player gets more resources in later stages, the costs of higher-tier crafting rise proportionally to prevent "over-farming" early zones.

---

### 3. Pity Systems & Bad Luck Protection

To prevent player frustration in a linear strategy game, **"The Entropy Tracker"** is implemented:

1.  **Void Shard Pity:** If a player goes 10 Common encounters without a Rare drop, the 11th encounter has a 50% increased chance to drop a Void Shard.
2.  **Blueprint Protection:** The game tracks which blueprints the player has. Loot tables will *never* drop a duplicate blueprint until the entire tier set is collected. If a duplicate is rolled, it is automatically converted into a large sum of **Galactic Credits**.
3.  **Resource Balancing:** If a player’s inventory is critically low on **Plasma Cells** (needed for travel), the drop rate for cells from Common enemies increases by 20% until a safety threshold is met.

---

### 4. Reward Structures

#### A. Quest Completion (The Linear Path)
Quests are the primary way players unlock new "Trade Routes" (Sectors).
*   **Main Story Quests:** Reward **Fixed Blueprints** and **Prototype Cores**. This ensures the player is never "too weak" to progress to the next sector due to bad RNG.
*   **Side Missions:** Reward **Galactic Credits** and **Star-Iron**. These are designed to help players "catch up" if they lost ships in combat.

#### B. Achievement Unlocks (Skill-Based)
Achievements provide **Permanent Passive Buffs** rather than raw resources.
*   *Example: "Master Miner" (Mine 1,000 Asteroids)* -> Reward: +5% Star-Iron yield permanently.
*   *Example: "Untouchable" (Defeat a Boss without losing a ship)* -> Reward: -10% Crafting Time.

#### C. Milestone Rewards (The "Checkpoints")
Every 5 levels of progression, the player receives a **"Galactic Supply Crate"**:
*   Contains a curated mix of all 4 resources.
*   Guarantees 1 **Epic** component.
*   Purpose: To provide a "power spike" right before a difficulty jump.

#### D. First-Time Bonuses
*   **First Boss Kill:** Guaranteed "Experimental Weapon" (Epic).
*   **First Craft:** 50% resource refund to teach the crafting mechanic.
*   **New Sector Entry:** A "Discovery Bonus" of Galactic Credits to encourage exploration.

---

### 5. Balance and Sinks

To ensure the loot system doesn't lead to hyper-inflation:

*   **The "Maintenance" Sink:** As players acquire more loot and build larger fleets, the **Plasma Cell** cost to maintain those ships scales. This forces players to continue engaging with the loot system rather than just hoarding.
*   **Inventory Caps:** Players must spend **Star-Iron** and **Credits** to upgrade cargo holds. If they don't invest in storage, excess loot is "vented into space" (destroyed), creating a meaningful choice between upgrading combat power or resource capacity.
*   **Linear Gating:** High-tier loot (Epic/Legendary) simply does not drop in early sectors. This prevents players from grinding low-level, easy content to bypass the difficulty of later stages.

## Monetization Strategy Design

To design a successful monetization strategy for **Galactic Trader**, we must first briefly define the four-resource economy that supports it.

### **The Core Economy (Quick Reference)**
1.  **Credits (Currency):** Earned via trade routes and missions. Used for ship maintenance and basic parts.
2.  **Raw Isotopes (Material):** Mined from planets. Used for crafting advanced ship components.
3.  **Data Cores (Progression/XP):** Recovered from ancient wrecks. Used to unlock the linear tech tree.
4.  **Dark Matter (Premium):** Purchased with real money or earned in tiny amounts via milestones.

---

## **Monetization Strategy: Galactic Trader**

### **1. Optional Purchases**
The goal is to offer "Quality of Life" (QoL) and "Time Compression" without making the game impossible for free players.

*   **Convenience Items:**
    *   **Cargo Expansion Modules ($4.99):** Permanent +20% increase to ship storage. This reduces the frequency of "return trips" but doesn't make the ship faster or stronger in combat.
    *   **Automated Mining Drones ($2.99/7 days):** Collects *Raw Isotopes* while the player is offline (up to a cap).
    *   **Fast-Travel Beacons ($0.99 for 5):** Allows instant travel to previously visited sectors, bypassing the real-time flight/navigation strategy.
*   **Time Savers:**
    *   **Data Overclockers ($1.99):** A 2-hour buff that doubles the *Data Core* (XP) gain from exploration.
    *   **Credit Stimulus ($4.99 - $19.99):** Direct purchase of *Credits*. Value is pegged to the player’s current progression tier to ensure it never fully bypasses the need to play.
*   **Price Points & Value:**
    *   **Micro-transactions ($0.99 - $4.99):** Consumables and small boosts.
    *   **Value Bundles ($14.99):** "The Explorer’s Starter Pack" – Includes a unique ship skin, a 7-day booster, and enough Dark Matter for one Battle Pass.

### **2. Battle Pass: "The Star-Charter’s Log"**
*   **Duration:** 60 Days (Seasonal).
*   **Price:** 1,000 Dark Matter (~$9.99 USD).
*   **Free Tier Rewards:** Small amounts of *Credits* and *Raw Isotopes*, basic ship decals, and one "Data Overclocker" every 10 levels.
*   **Premium Tier Rewards:** Exclusive ship hulls, "Legendary" engine trails, significant *Dark Matter* rebates (enough to buy the next pass if completed), and unique interior cockpit decorations.
*   **Estimated Completion Time:** 40–50 hours of active gameplay.
*   **Value Proposition:** The Battle Pass offers roughly $50 worth of items for $10, incentivizing daily logins and consistent engagement with the linear progression system.

### **3. Cosmetics**
Cosmetics are the primary revenue driver, allowing players to express their status in the galaxy without affecting the competitive balance of the strategy.

*   **Categories:**
    *   **Ship Hulls (Skins):** Complete model swaps or high-quality texture changes.
    *   **Engine Trails:** Changes the color and particle effects of the ship’s exhaust (e.g., Blue Ion, Red Plasma, Rainbow).
    *   **Cockpit Bobbleheads:** Interactive 3D items for the first-person flight view.
    *   **UI Themes:** Custom HUD colors and sound sets.
*   **Rarity Tiers:**
    *   **Common (Grey):** Simple color swaps.
    *   **Rare (Blue):** Patterned skins with metallic finishes.
    *   **Epic (Purple):** Animated textures (e.g., shifting nebula patterns).
    *   **Legendary (Gold):** Unique geometry (e.g., a ship that looks like an ancient relic).
*   **Pricing Strategy:**
    *   Direct Purchase via the "Galactic Boutique." Prices range from $2.00 (Common) to $15.00 (Legendary).

### **4. Pay-to-Win (P2W) Risk Assessment**

 Purchase Type | Risk Level | Potential Issue | Safeguard |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Resource Doublers** | Medium | Players might reach the "End Game" too quickly, trivializing the linear progression. | Boosters only affect *acquisition rate*, not *max capacity*. Players still need to engage with the strategy to spend resources. |
 **Direct Credit Sales** | Low | Could allow players to buy the best gear immediately. | High-tier gear is "Tech Locked." Even with infinite Credits, a player cannot buy a Tier 5 engine until they have earned enough *Data Cores* (XP) through gameplay. |
 **Mining Drones** | Low | Offline progress might discourage active play. | Drones have a 24-hour fuel limit and lower efficiency than manual mining. They are a supplement, not a replacement. |
 **Cargo Expansion** | Low | Gives a mathematical advantage in trading efficiency. | Expansion is capped. Free players can earn similar (though slightly smaller) expansions through the linear tech tree. |

### **Final Recommendation for Balance:**
To maintain the integrity of *Galactic Trader*, the **Data Cores (Progression Resource)** should remain the most protected resource. While players can buy "Overclockers" to earn them faster, they should **never** be able to buy Data Cores directly. This ensures that even the highest-spending players must actually play the game, explore the galaxy, and master the strategy to progress through the story and unlock new sectors.

## Engagement Systems Design

This engagement system is designed for **Galactic Trader**, a linear strategy game focused on resource management and crafting. Since player-to-player trading is disabled, these systems focus on the player’s relationship with the game world and their personal progression milestones.

---

### 1. Daily Rewards: The "Logistics Check-in"
To encourage daily logins, the system rewards the player for maintaining their "Trade Fleet" readiness.

*   **Reward Structure**: A 7-day rotating calendar.
    *   *Days 1-3:* Small bundles of **Raw Ore**.
    *   *Days 4-5:* **Credits** and mid-tier **Refined Components**.
    *   *Day 6:* A "Data Cache" containing a significant amount of **Tech Data**.
    *   *Day 7:* A "Prototype Crate" (contains a rare crafting blueprint or a large sum of all resources).
*   **Streak Bonuses**: Completing a full 7-day streak grants a "Efficiency Buffer" for the following week, reducing crafting times by 10%.
*   **Catch-up Mechanics**: Players can spend **Credits** (the primary currency sink) to "Back-fill" a missed day, ensuring they don't lose their Day 7 reward.
*   **Value Scaling**: Rewards are tiered based on the player’s current Sector (Progression Level). A Level 50 player receives 10x the Ore of a Level 1 player to ensure the rewards remain relevant.
*   **Retention Impact**: High. It establishes a low-friction daily habit and provides the "missing piece" for crafting projects.

---

### 2. Seasonal Content: "Galactic Eras"
Seasons provide a thematic wrapper for linear progression, introducing new "Tech Trees" that players must climb.

*   **Season Duration**: 90 Days (Quarterly).
*   **Seasonal Themes**: Examples include *“The Silicon Gold Rush”* (focus on electronics crafting) or *“The Void Frontier”* (focus on long-range engine tech).
*   **Exclusive Rewards**: 
    *   Unique ship hulls (cosmetic or minor stat variations).
    *   "Legacy Blueprints" that allow crafting of unique Refined Components not available in the base game.
*   **Seasonal Progression**: A "Milestone Path." Players earn "Season XP" by spending resources and completing crafting projects. This is a linear track that mirrors the core game’s progression but offers accelerated rewards.
*   **Retention Impact**: Medium-Long Term. It prevents the "end-game plateau" by giving veteran players new blueprints to chase every three months.

---

### 3. Events: "Sector Anomalies"
Events create short-term urgency and break the routine of standard resource gathering.

*   **Event Types**:
    *   **Asteroid Pulse (Recurring - 48 hours):** Double **Raw Ore** yield from all sources.
    *   **Tech Breakthrough (Special - 1 week):** Crafting **Refined Components** requires 20% fewer resources.
    *   **Deep Space Signal (Limited-Time):** A unique mission chain that yields massive **Tech Data** but requires specific high-tier crafted items to enter.
*   **Event Frequency**: One mini-event every weekend; one major event per month.
*   **Participation Incentives**: "Event Tokens" that can be spent in a temporary shop for rare materials, bypassing the usual linear grind.
*   **Balance with Core Gameplay**: Events are designed to alleviate "bottlenecks." If data shows players are struggling to move from Tier 2 to Tier 3, a "Tech Breakthrough" event is triggered to help the player base progress.

---

### 4. Retention Mechanics: "The Fleet Command"
These systems focus on long-term attachment and bringing players back after a hiatus.

*   **Login Bonuses (The "Daily Shipment")**: Beyond the 7-day streak, a passive "Shipment" accumulates while the player is offline (up to 24 hours). This ensures that even a 5-minute session feels productive.
*   **Comeback Rewards ("Re-Activation Protocol")**: If a player is inactive for >14 days, they receive a "Resource Injection" upon return—a massive bundle of **Raw Ore** and **Credits** to help them immediately craft the next upgrade in their linear path, reducing the "re-learning" friction.
*   **Social Features (Fleet Alliances)**: While there is no trading, players can join "Alliances." 
    *   *Shared Goals:* If the Alliance collectively crafts 10,000 components, everyone gets a 5% discount on **Tech Data** costs.
    *   *Ghost Trading:* Players can "donate" resources to a communal Alliance Project (e.g., building a Space Station) which provides passive buffs to all members.
*   **Retention Impact**: Social features provide a sense of accountability and community, while comeback rewards prevent "progression despair" where a player feels they have fallen too far behind the curve.

---

### Summary Table: Engagement Impact

 System | Frequency | Primary Reward | Retention Goal |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Daily Rewards** | Daily | Materials/Credits | Short-term habit formation. |
 **Seasonal Content** | 90 Days | Blueprints/Skins | Long-term goal setting. |
 **Events** | Weekly | Resource Boosts | Breaking monotony/Urgency. |
 **Fleet Alliances** | Continuous | Passive Buffs | Community & social pressure. |
 **Comeback Pack** | One-time | Bulk Resources | Reducing churn/re-entry friction. |

**Balance Note:** To ensure these systems don't break the linear progression, all rewards are "Level-Locked." A player cannot receive a Tier 4 Refined Component from a Daily Reward if they are only at Tier 1 of the tech tree. This maintains the integrity of the strategy and progression.

## Economy Forecast (6 months)

This design for **Galactic Trader** focuses on a tightly controlled, linear progression loop where "trading" refers to NPC-based commerce and resource conversion rather than a player-to-player market.

---

# Part 1: Resource System Design

### 1. Resource Identity
 Resource | Type | Purpose | Thematic Fit |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Galactic Credits (GC)** | Currency | Standard medium for NPC transactions, fuel, and repairs. | The universal grease of the galaxy. |
 **Star-Iron** | Material | Primary bulk material for hull upgrades and base components. | Raw ore mined from asteroids. |
 **Data Cores** | Experience/Tech | Used to unlock new blueprints and "level up" tech tiers. | Salvaged ancient knowledge. |
 **Void Crystals** | Premium/Rare | Used for instant travel, rare skins, and endgame "Overclocks." | Rare, unstable energy source. |

### 2. Generation Sources
*   **GC:** Earned via mission rewards (1,000–5,000 GC) and selling surplus Star-Iron to NPC depots.
*   **Star-Iron:** Acquired via mining mini-games. Yields 50–200 units per asteroid. Scaling: Better lasers increase yield by 10% per tier.
*   **Data Cores:** Found in derelict ships or as rare mission rewards (1–5 per activity).
*   **Void Crystals:** Daily login rewards (5), high-difficulty boss encounters (10), or IAP.

### 3. Consumption Uses
*   **GC:** Fuel (Low cost, high frequency), Repairs (Medium cost), NPC Crew Salaries.
*   **Star-Iron:** Crafting ship parts (500–5,000 units). High volume, medium frequency.
*   **Data Cores:** Researching new tiers (10–100 cores). Low frequency, high value.
*   **Void Crystals:** Reducing crafting timers or purchasing "Exotic" ship variants.

### 4. Storage and Limits
*   **Storage:** Star-Iron is limited by Cargo Hold capacity. GC and Data Cores are digital and unlimited.
*   **Rationale:** Cargo limits force players to return to hubs, creating a gameplay rhythm of "Expedition -> Return -> Craft."
*   **Overflow:** Excess Star-Iron must be jettisoned or sold at a loss if the hold is full.

### 5. Exchange Rates (NPC Only)
*   **100 Star-Iron : 10 GC** (Selling raw materials).
*   **500 GC : 1 Data Core** (Buying tech from black markets - limited stock).
*   **Void Crystals** cannot be bought with GC (Hard gate).

### 6. Sink Mechanisms
*   **The "Entropy" Sink:** Ship maintenance and fuel costs scale with ship size, ensuring high-level players still need to manage GC.
*   **The "Refinement" Sink:** Crafting high-tier alloys requires "burning" large amounts of Star-Iron to prevent inventory bloat.

# Part 2: 6-Month Economy Forecast

### Month 1: The Launch Phase
*   **Player Progression:** Avg. Level 10. 0% Endgame. Skill tree 15% complete.
*   **Resource Abundance:** Star-Iron is scarce; players spend GC as fast as they earn it on basic upgrades.
*   **Economy Health:** **Healthy.** High velocity. Players are "hungry" for all resources.
*   **Adjustments:** Monitor early-game "Star-Iron" bottlenecks. If players stall at Level 5, increase asteroid density in starting zones.

### Month 2: The Industrial Expansion
*   **Player Progression:** Avg. Level 25. 2% (Hardcore) reaching mid-game plateaus.
*   **Resource Abundance:** Star-Iron holdings stabilize as cargo holds grow. Data Core scarcity begins to bite.
*   **Economy Health:** **Healthy.** Hardcore players begin accumulating GC.
*   **Adjustments:** Introduce "Bulk Crafting" recipes to drain Star-Iron surpluses.

### Month 3: The Tech Wall
*   **Player Progression:** Avg. Level 40. 10% reaching early endgame.
*   **Resource Abundance:** GC inflation begins for Hardcore players (holding 1M+). Data Cores are the primary progress gate.
*   **Economy Health:** **Slightly Inflated (GC).**
*   **Adjustments:** Increase GC costs for high-tier ship repairs. Introduce "Data Core Scanners" (GC sink) to help players find rare tech.

### Month 4: The Void Crisis (Content Patch)
*   **Player Progression:** Avg. Level 50. 25% at endgame. Skill tree 70% complete.
*   **Resource Abundance:** Star-Iron becomes a "nuisance" resource. Void Crystals usage peaks as players "Overclock" gear.
*   **Economy Health:** **Stable.** New content provides new sinks.
*   **Adjustments:** Add "Star-Iron to Fuel" conversion to give the base material utility for endgame players who no longer need to craft basic parts.

### Month 5: Wealth Disparity
*   **Player Progression:** Avg. Level 55. 45% at endgame.
*   **Resource Abundance:** Hardcore players have 10x the wealth of Casuals. Wealth distribution is heavily skewed.
*   **Economy Health:** **Inflated.** GC has lost value for the top 10%.
*   **Adjustments:** Introduce "Galactic Monuments" or "Base Customization"—purely cosmetic GC sinks that cost millions but offer prestige.

### Month 6: Maturity & Prestige
*   **Player Progression:** Avg. Level 60 (Cap). 60% at endgame. Skill tree 95% complete.
*   **Resource Abundance:** Velocity slows. Players hoard Void Crystals for future expansions.
*   **Economy Health:** **Deflated (Engagement).** Without new sinks, players stop gathering.
*   **Adjustments:** Implement a "Prestige/Rebirth" mechanic where players trade all resources for permanent stat bonuses, resetting the economy for the individual.
