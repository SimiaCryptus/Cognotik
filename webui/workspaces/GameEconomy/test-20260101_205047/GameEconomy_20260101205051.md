# Game Economy Design: Galactic Trader

## Game Economy Design

**Game:** Galactic Trader

**Type:** strategy

**Progression Style:** linear

## Resource System Design

This resource system for **Galactic Trader** is designed as a "Closed-Loop Production Economy." Since trading with external NPCs or players is disabled, the "Trading" aspect of the title refers to the internal logistics of converting raw materials into progression milestones.

The economy relies on a **Linear-Vertical** progression model where resources from the previous tier are required to unlock the next, ensuring players must maintain their entire infrastructure as they expand.

---

### 1. Resource: Raw Ore (Material)
*   **Resource Identity**: The foundational building block. It represents unrefined minerals, silicates, and metals harvested from asteroid belts.
*   **Generation Sources**: 
    *   **Mining Drills**: Passive generation over time.
    *   **Manual Extraction**: Active clicking/tapping on asteroids (early game).
    *   **Scaling**: Higher-tier sectors provide "Rich Veins" which multiply the Ore per second (1x, 5x, 25x).
*   **Consumption Uses**: 
    *   **Refining**: The primary ingredient for *Advanced Alloys*.
    *   **Maintenance**: Small amounts are consumed to repair ship hull integrity.
    *   **Base Building**: Used in large quantities for initial structure placement.
*   **Storage and Limits**: 
    *   **Cargo Bays**: Fixed capacity. Once full, mining stops.
    *   **Rationale**: Forces the player to engage with the crafting system rather than hoarding raw materials.
*   **Exchange Rates**: Cannot be bought. Converted to *Advanced Alloys* at a 10:1 ratio in the Refinery.
*   **Sink Mechanisms**: Hull repairs act as a constant, low-level drain. Large-scale "Mega-Structures" in the late game require massive Ore dumps to complete.

---

### 2. Resource: Fusion Cells (Energy/Action)
*   **Resource Identity**: The "Fuel" of the economy. It represents the energy required to power refineries, engines, and life support.
*   **Generation Sources**: 
    *   **Solar Arrays**: Constant generation based on proximity to stars.
    *   **Gas Siphons**: High-yield, high-risk extraction from gas giants.
    *   **Frequency**: Generated every second; fluctuates based on ship location.
*   **Consumption Uses**: 
    *   **Crafting Cost**: Every crafting recipe (Refining) requires a flat amount of Fusion Cells.
    *   **Travel**: Moving between sectors costs Cells based on distance.
    *   **Overclocking**: Can be spent to temporarily double the speed of Ore production.
*   **Storage and Limits**: 
    *   **Battery Banks**: Limited capacity. 
    *   **Overflow**: Excess energy is vented (lost), encouraging players to always keep their refineries running.
*   **Exchange Rates**: 100 Cells + 10 Ore = 1 *Advanced Alloy*.
*   **Sink Mechanisms**: Travel costs scale exponentially with distance, preventing players from skipping linear progression steps without significant energy infrastructure.

---

### 3. Resource: Advanced Alloys (Crafted Material)
*   **Resource Identity**: The mid-tier "Complexity" resource. It represents processed, high-grade materials used for technological advancement.
*   **Generation Sources**: 
    *   **Refinery (Crafting)**: The **only** way to obtain Alloys is by combining *Raw Ore* and *Fusion Cells*.
    *   **Success Rate**: Initially 100%, but late-game "Experimental Alloys" may have a failure rate unless upgraded.
*   **Consumption Uses**: 
    *   **Ship Upgrades**: Upgrading engines, drills, and storage.
    *   **Tech Unlocks**: Required in batches to unlock new crafting recipes.
    *   **Progression Gates**: Used to build "Warp Gates" to reach new sectors.
*   **Storage and Limits**: 
    *   **Secure Vaults**: Very limited storage compared to Ore. 
    *   **Rationale**: Prevents the player from "pre-farming" the entire game's progression in the starting zone.
*   **Exchange Rates**: 50 Alloys + 500 Cells = 1 *Command Data*.
*   **Sink Mechanisms**: High-tier ship equipment has an "Ablative" quality, requiring Alloys for permanent stat maintenance or "Refurbishing."

---

### 4. Resource: Command Data (Progression/Experience)
*   **Resource Identity**: The ultimate progression resource. It represents the navigational data, blueprints, and "Galactic Intel" needed to "win" the linear campaign.
*   **Generation Sources**: 
    *   **The Laboratory (Crafting)**: Created by processing *Advanced Alloys* and *Fusion Cells*.
    *   **Mission Milestones**: Awarded for reaching new sectors or completing specific strategy objectives.
*   **Consumption Uses**: 
    *   **Linear Progression**: Spent on the "Galactic Map" to unlock the next star system.
    *   **Permanent Perks**: Spent on the "Command Tree" (e.g., +10% Mining Speed, -5% Fuel Consumption).
*   **Storage and Limits**: 
    *   **No Limit**: Stored in the ship's mainframe. 
    *   **Rationale**: Acts as the player's "Score" and permanent progression marker.
*   **Exchange Rates**: This is the "End-of-Line" resource. It cannot be converted back into lower-tier materials.
*   **Sink Mechanisms**: The cost of unlocking new sectors increases sharply (e.g., 10, 100, 1000). This ensures the player must scale their Ore and Alloy production to keep up with the linear path.

---

### Economy Summary & Balance

 Resource | Role | Primary Source | Primary Sink |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Raw Ore** | Foundation | Mining | Crafting Alloys |
 **Fusion Cells** | Action/Time | Solar/Gas | Crafting & Travel |
 **Advanced Alloys** | Complexity | Crafting (Ore+Cells) | Upgrades & Data |
 **Command Data** | Progression | Crafting (Alloy+Cells) | Unlocking Sectors |

**How it creates meaningful choices:**
The player is constantly balancing **Expansion vs. Efficiency**. 
*   Do I spend my *Fusion Cells* to travel to a new asteroid belt (Expansion)? 
*   Or do I spend them in the Refinery to turn my *Ore* into *Alloys* (Efficiency)? 
*   Since there is no trading, the player cannot "buy" their way out of a resource bottleneck; they must optimize their logistics chain to ensure *Ore* flows into *Alloys*, which flows into *Data*.

**Monetization Support:**
In a strategy context, monetization would focus on **Fusion Cell Refills** (Time-savers) or **Storage Expansion** (Convenience), allowing players to bypass the storage limits that force frequent crafting cycles.

## Progression System Design

This design for **Galactic Trader** focuses on a "Production Strategy" model. Despite the title, the "Trading" aspect is handled through **Contract Fulfillment** (crafting and delivering) rather than a live market, adhering to the "Trading: False" constraint.

## Part 1: Resource System Design

### 1. Resource Identity
 Resource | Type | Purpose | Thematic Fit |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Scrap Metal** | Material | Basic building block for all ship components and station modules. | Salvaged from debris fields. |
 **Helium-3** | Fuel/Energy | Powers ship movement, crafting refineries, and life support. | Harvested from gas giants. |
 **Neural Data** | Experience | Used to "level up" the player and unlock new blueprints. | Gathered via scanning and exploration. |
 **Void Shards** | Rare/Premium | Used for instant crafting, high-tier overclocks, and cosmetic hulls. | Found in deep-space anomalies. |

### 2. Generation & Consumption
*   **Scrap Metal:** Acquired via Mining Lasers (10-50 per node). Spent on Hull repairs and Tier 1-2 crafting.
*   **Helium-3:** Acquired via Gas Scoops (5 per minute). Spent on every jump between sectors and powering the "Fabricator."
*   **Neural Data:** Acquired by completing "Trade Contracts" (delivering crafted goods). Spent automatically to progress levels.
*   **Void Shards:** Acquired via Daily Milestones or rare "Black Hole" events (1-5 per event). Spent on skipping wait times or Tier 5 "Exotic" gear.

### 3. Storage & Sinks
*   **Storage:** Limited by "Cargo Bay" capacity. Players must upgrade bays using Scrap Metal to hold more.
*   **Sinks:** 
    *   **Maintenance:** Ships degrade over time, requiring Scrap Metal.
    *   **Refining Loss:** Converting raw Scrap into "Hardened Steel" (Crafting) loses 20% of the volume.
    *   **Fuel Burn:** High-speed travel consumes Helium-3 exponentially.

## Part 3: Skill Trees

Players earn **1 Skill Point per level** (Total: 30). There are 3 branches.

### Branch 1: The Extractor (Resource Acquisition)
*   **Deep Drill:** Increases Scrap Metal yield by 5% per rank (5 ranks).
*   **Gas Siphon:** Helium-3 harvesting is 10% faster (5 ranks).
*   **Synergy:** If both are maxed, unlocks "Asteroid Cracker" (Chance to find Void Shards while mining).

### Branch 2: The Fabricator (Crafting & Economy)
*   **Material Thrift:** Reduces Scrap Metal cost in crafting by 5% (5 ranks).
*   **Overclock:** Reduces crafting time by 10% (5 ranks).
*   **Synergy:** If both are maxed, unlocks "Masterwork" (5% chance to craft a double-strength item).

### Branch 3: The Navigator (Exploration & Speed)
*   **Fuel Efficiency:** Reduces Helium-3 consumption by 5% (5 ranks).
*   **Data Link:** Increases Neural Data gained from contracts by 10% (5 ranks).
*   **Synergy:** If both are maxed, unlocks "Slipstream" (Instant travel between discovered sectors).

### Respec Mechanics
*   **Cost:** Respecing costs **Void Shards**. 
*   **Scaling:** The first respec is 10 Shards, doubling each time (20, 40, 80...). This encourages players to commit to a build while allowing for late-game pivots.

---

## Part 4: Design Philosophy Summary
*   **Meaningful Choices:** Players must choose between being a "Miner" (high raw resources) or a "Fabricator" (high efficiency). Since storage is limited, you cannot hoard everything; you must decide what to craft and when.
*   **Linear Progression:** The linear XP curve ensures that while the game gets harder, the goals always feel attainable. The "Milestone Ships" every 5-10 levels provide the necessary power spikes to handle new sectors.
*   **No Trading:** By removing player-to-player trading, the economy remains stable. Progression is tied strictly to the player's ability to manage their own supply chain and fulfill NPC contracts.

## Loot & Reward System Design

This design for **Galactic Trader** focuses on a closed-loop economy where "trading" refers to the logistical fulfillment of contracts and crafting rather than a player-to-player market. The loot system is designed to fuel a linear progression path, ensuring players always have the materials needed for the next tier of ship upgrades and station modules.

---

### 1. Resource Context Recap
To ensure the loot system functions, we define the 4 core resources:
1.  **Star-Iron (Common Material):** Used for hull plating and basic structures.
2.  **Plasma Cores (Rare Material):** Used for engines, weapons, and high-tech crafting.
3.  **Credits (Currency):** Used for assembly costs, crew wages, and fuel.
4.  **Data Fragments (Research/XP):** Used to unlock new crafting blueprints and sector access.

---

### 2. Loot Tables & Drop Logic

#### A. Common Activities (Scouts, Asteroid Mining, Small Freighters)
*Focus: High-volume, low-rarity materials for consistent crafting.*
*   **Star-Iron:** 70% chance (10–50 units)
*   **Credits:** 20% chance (50–100 units)
*   **Data Fragments:** 9% chance (1–2 units)
*   **Plasma Cores:** 1% chance (1 unit)
*   **Scaling:** Quantity increases by 15% per sector level.

#### B. Elite Challenges (Heavy Cruisers, Pirate Dens, Nebula Anomalies)
*Focus: Mid-tier components and rare materials.*
*   **Star-Iron:** 40% chance (100–200 units)
*   **Plasma Cores:** 35% chance (2–5 units)
*   **Credits:** 15% chance (500–1,000 units)
*   **Rare Blueprints (Consumable):** 10% chance (Unlocks one-time high-efficiency craft)
*   **Scaling:** Higher sectors introduce "Refined" versions of materials with 2x value.

#### C. Boss Encounters (Capital Ships, Ancient Gatekeepers)
*Focus: Major progression leaps and guaranteed rare drops.*
*   **Plasma Cores:** 100% chance (10–20 units)
*   **Data Fragments:** 100% chance (Large cache: 50–100 units)
*   **Epic Blueprint:** 100% chance (Permanent unlock for a new Ship Class or Module)
*   **Legendary Component:** 5% chance (Unique item: "Ever-Burning Reactor" or "Void Shield")

---

### 3. Pity Systems & Bad Luck Protection

To prevent "progression stalls" in a linear strategy game, Galactic Trader employs the **"Salvage Calibration"** system:
*   **Plasma Core Pity:** For every 10 Elite enemies killed without a Plasma Core drop, the drop rate increases by 10% cumulatively until one drops.
*   **Blueprint Guarantee:** If a player completes 5 missions in a sector without finding a new Blueprint, the next "Elite" encounter is guaranteed to drop a "Missing Tech" fragment.
*   **Resource Recycling:** Players can dismantle unwanted Common items to receive 25% of the Star-Iron cost back, ensuring no loot is truly "trash."

---

### 4. Reward Structures

#### Quest Completion (The "Contract" System)
Since trading is internal, quests act as "Trade Contracts."
*   **Primary Story Quests:** Reward **Data Fragments** and **Unique Blueprints**. These are the primary gates for linear progression.
*   **Side Logistics Quests:** Reward **Credits** and **Star-Iron**. These allow players to "grind" resources if they over-extended their fleet.
*   **Urgent Distress Calls:** High-risk, high-reward. Usually rewards a large burst of **Plasma Cores**.

#### Achievement Unlocks (The "Pilot’s Log")
Achievements provide one-time permanent account boosts rather than raw resources.
*   *Example: "Master Miner" (Mine 10,000 Star-Iron):* Increases Star-Iron drop quantity by 10% permanently.
*   *Example: "Void Hunter" (Destroy 50 Elites):* Increases Plasma Core drop rate by 2%.

#### Milestone Rewards (Tier Progression)
When a player reaches a new Sector or Ship Tier:
*   **The "Expansion Pack":** A massive bundle of all 4 resources (enough to build the first basic ship of the new tier).
*   **New Tech Access:** Unlocks the ability for Common enemies in the *new* sector to drop materials that were previously "Rare" in the *old* sector.

#### First-Time Bonuses
*   **First Boss Kill:** Grants a "Prototype Module" that does not require crafting materials, giving the player an immediate power spike to handle the next difficulty tier.
*   **First Craft:** Refunds 50% of the materials used to encourage players to engage with the crafting system early.

---

### 5. Scaling & Balance Strategy

 Progression Stage | Primary Loot Source | Bottleneck Resource | Reward Strategy |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Early Game** | Common Enemies | Star-Iron | High drop rates to allow rapid building/experimentation. |
 **Mid Game** | Elite Challenges | Plasma Cores | Focus on "Pity Drops" to ensure the player can upgrade their engine/weapons. |
 **Late Game** | Bosses/Contracts | Data Fragments | Rewards shift toward "Efficiency Upgrades" and massive credit sinks. |

### 6. Summary of Design Goals
*   **Meaningful Choices:** Players must choose between hunting Elites for Plasma Cores (High Risk) or farming Commons for Star-Iron (Low Risk).
*   **Linear Support:** Loot quantities scale exactly with the cost of the next tier of ships, preventing the player from becoming "stuck" in an old sector.
*   **Anti-Frustration:** The Pity System ensures that even with poor RNG, the player will eventually gather the materials needed to progress the story.

## Monetization Strategy Design

To design a monetization strategy for **Galactic Trader**, we must first establish the four-resource economy that supports it. Since player-to-player trading is disabled, the economy focuses on the player’s relationship with the environment and NPC factions.

### Part 1: The Resource Economy

1.  **Credits (Currency):** Earned via NPC contracts. Used for fuel, repairs, and basic components.
2.  **Alloy Scraps (Material):** Salvaged from debris. Used in the **Crafting** system to build ship modules.
3.  **Data Cores (Progression):** Found in anomalies. Spent on the Tech Tree to unlock new sectors and blueprints.
4.  **Nova Crystals (Premium):** Purchased with real money or earned via rare milestones. Used for cosmetics and QoL.

---

### Part 2: Monetization Strategy

#### 1. Optional Purchases (Convenience & Time Savers)
The goal is to reduce friction for busy players without allowing them to "buy" victory in the linear progression.

*   **Cargo Expansion ($4.99 per tier):** Increases the storage limit for Alloy Scraps and Data Cores. This reduces the frequency of returning to base, allowing for longer exploration sorties.
*   **Automated Salvage Drones ($2.99/7 days):** A temporary "worker" that collects a small amount of Alloy Scraps while the player is offline. It scales with the player’s current sector to ensure it remains relevant but never exceeds active play rewards.
*   **Jump-Drive Overclock (Time Saver - $0.99 or Nova Crystals):** Instantly completes a long-distance travel or a crafting timer. 
*   **Resource Refiners (Doublers - $9.99):** A permanent 1.5x multiplier to Alloy Scrap gains. This is priced as a "one-time buy" to act as a "Premium Edition" feel for dedicated players.

#### 2. Battle Pass: "The Frontier Ledger"
The Battle Pass provides a steady stream of goals to keep the linear progression engaging.

*   **Duration:** 60 Days (Seasonal).
*   **Price:** 1,000 Nova Crystals (~$9.99).
*   **Free Tier Rewards:** Basic Credits, small amounts of Alloy Scraps, and one "Rare" ship decal at level 50.
*   **Premium Tier Rewards:** Exclusive Ship Hulls (visual only), Engine Trail effects, large bundles of Data Cores (to speed up Tech Tree progression), and enough Nova Crystals to purchase the next pass if completed.
*   **Estimated Completion Time:** 40–50 hours of active gameplay.
*   **Value Proposition:** The total value of items in the Premium Tier is roughly $60, providing a 6x return on investment for active players.

#### 3. Cosmetics (Self-Expression)
Since *Galactic Trader* is a strategy game, cosmetics focus on the visual representation of the player's fleet and UI.

*   **Categories:**
    *   **Ship Hulls:** Complete model swaps for your trading vessels (e.g., "Stealth Freighter," "Golden Galleon").
    *   **Engine Trails:** Changes the color and shape of exhaust (e.g., Blue Ion, Red Plasma, Rainbow).
    *   **Bridge Interiors:** Static backgrounds for the menu/management screens.
    *   **Holographic Pets:** Small drones that hover near your ship in the hangar.
*   **Rarity Tiers:**
    *   **Common (Blue):** Simple color swaps ($1.99).
    *   **Epic (Purple):** Animated textures or minor model changes ($5.99).
    *   **Legendary (Gold):** Unique geometry and custom sound effects ($14.99).
*   **Acquisition:** Direct purchase via the "Nova Store" or as milestone rewards in the Battle Pass.

#### 4. Pay-to-Win Risk Assessment & Safeguards

 Purchase Type | P2W Risk | Mitigation Strategy |
 :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Data Core Bundles** | **High** | Data Cores are capped by "Sector Gates." Even if a player buys cores, they cannot use them to unlock Tier 4 tech while they are still in a Tier 1 sector. |
 **Resource Doublers** | **Medium** | The 1.5x multiplier is significant but does not grant access to exclusive items. Crafting still requires the player to find specific "Blueprint Fragments" which cannot be purchased. |
 **Cargo Expansion** | **Low** | This is a pure Quality-of-Life (QoL) improvement. It allows for more efficiency but does not increase the power of the ship's weapons or engines. |
 **Instant Crafting** | **Low** | Since there is no PvP and no player-driven market, a player finishing a ship module faster does not negatively impact the experience of others. |

**Final Safeguard:** 
The "Linear Progression" design ensures that all players must pass through the same narrative and difficulty milestones. Premium purchases act as "grease" for the wheels of progression rather than a "skip" button. No exclusive power-granting items (e.g., a ship with 200% more health) will ever be sold for Nova Crystals; all "Premium Ships" are visual skins for hulls that must be earned through gameplay.

## Engagement Systems Design

This engagement system is designed for **Galactic Trader**, a linear strategy game. Since player-to-player trading is disabled, these systems focus on the player’s relationship with the **Galactic Market (NPCs)** and their own **Fleet Progression**.

---

### 1. Daily Rewards: The "Logistics Check-In"
To encourage daily logins, the system simulates a "Daily Supply Drop" from the player’s home faction.

*   **Reward Structure (7-Day Rotating Cycle):**
    *   **Days 1-2:** Common Materials (Scrap Metal/Fuel).
    *   **Days 3-5:** Mid-tier Crafting Components or Currency (Credits).
    *   **Day 6:** Rare Blueprints or Boosters (e.g., +20% Salvage Speed for 2 hours).
    *   **Day 7:** "High-Yield Crate" containing a small amount of Exotic Matter (Premium/Rare resource).
*   **Streak Bonuses:** Every consecutive 7-day completion adds a +5% permanent (stacking up to 25%) bonus to the *quantity* of resources in the next week’s drops.
*   **Catch-up Mechanics:** Players can spend a moderate amount of **Credits** (Standard Currency) to "Recover Missed Shipments," allowing them to maintain their streak if they miss a day.
*   **Value Scaling:** Rewards are tied to the player’s **Trade Rank**. A Rank 1 player gets 100 Scrap; a Rank 50 player gets 10,000 Scrap. This ensures the rewards remain relevant throughout the linear progression.
*   **Retention Impact:** High short-term retention. It establishes a "habit loop" where the player checks their fleet status every morning.

---

### 2. Seasonal Content: "Galactic Eras"
Seasons provide a "meta-layer" to the linear progression, offering temporary goals that don't disrupt the core balance.

*   **Season Duration:** 90 Days (Quarterly).
*   **Seasonal Themes:**
    *   *Example: "The Dyson Expansion"* – Focuses on energy resource gathering and building massive solar structures.
    *   *Example: "The Void Incursion"* – Focuses on combat salvage and defensive ship upgrades.
*   **Exclusive Rewards:**
    *   **Cosmetic:** Unique ship hull patterns and engine trail colors.
    *   **Functional:** "Legacy Modules"—ship parts that have unique stats but cannot be crafted outside the season.
*   **Seasonal Progression (The "Trade Log"):** A 50-level progression track. Players earn "Log Points" by completing standard missions and crafting items. 
    *   *Free Track:* Basic resources and blueprints.
    *   *Premium Track:* Accelerated resource gains and exclusive cosmetics.
*   **Retention Impact:** High long-term retention. It prevents the linear progression from feeling "stale" by introducing new thematic flavor every three months.

---

### 3. Events: "Market Fluctuations & Anomalies"
Events create urgency and break the routine of the standard resource-gathering loop.

*   **Event Types:**
    *   **Flash Market (Limited-Time):** For 4 hours, a "Black Market Trader" appears, offering high-tier crafting materials for 50% less Credits.
    *   **Resource Surge (Recurring):** Every weekend, a specific sector of space sees a "Plasma Storm," doubling the acquisition rate of Energy resources.
    *   **Community Milestone (Special):** A 10-day event where all players contribute Scrap Metal to a "Galactic Gateway" project. If the goal is met, everyone unlocks a permanent discount on travel costs.
*   **Frequency:** 
    *   *Minor:* Twice weekly (Weekends/Mid-week).
    *   *Major:* Once per month.
*   **Participation Incentives:** "Event Tokens" that can be traded for high-end crafting recipes that are otherwise locked behind late-game progression.
*   **Retention Impact:** Medium-term engagement. These events act as "appointment gaming" triggers, bringing players back at specific times.

---

### 4. Retention Mechanics: "The Syndicate System"
Since direct trading is disabled, social features focus on cooperation and competition through indirect means.

*   **Login Bonuses (The "Rested Bonus"):** If a player has been away for more than 24 hours, they gain "Optimized Crew" status, granting a 1.5x multiplier to their next 3 crafting tasks or missions.
*   **Comeback Rewards:** Players absent for 14+ days receive a "Re-activation Package" containing enough fuel and scrap to immediately jump back into the highest-level zone they have unlocked.
*   **Social Features (Trade Syndicates):**
    *   **Guilds/Clans:** Players join "Syndicates." While they can't trade resources, they share a **"Syndicate Tech Tree."**
    *   Members contribute resources to unlock passive buffs for everyone (e.g., +10% Cargo Space).
    *   **Leaderboards:** Weekly rankings for "Most Credits Earned" or "Deepest Space Explored," rewarding top players with prestige titles and small amounts of Exotic Matter.
*   **Frequency/Duration:** Permanent systems.
*   **Retention Impact:** Very high. The "Syndicate" creates social pressure and a sense of belonging, while the "Rested Bonus" reduces the guilt/friction of returning after a break.

---

### Summary of Balance & Integration

 System | Primary Resource Focus | Retention Goal | Balance Strategy |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Daily Rewards** | All (Low Volume) | Daily Habit | Scales with Trade Rank to prevent obsolescence. |
 **Seasonal Content** | Exotic/Cosmetic | 3-Month LTV | Seasonal items are side-grades, not power-creep. |
 **Events** | Credits/Materials | Weekly Spikes | Uses "Sink" mechanics to drain excess currency. |
 **Syndicates** | Social/Passive Buffs | Community Bond | No direct trading prevents "Gold Farming" or economy abuse. |

By combining these systems, **Galactic Trader** ensures that players have a reason to log in today (Daily Rewards), a reason to play this weekend (Events), and a reason to stay committed for the next year (Seasons and Syndicates).

## Economy Forecast (6 months)

This design for **Galactic Trader** focuses on a tightly controlled, linear progression economy where "trading" is themed as fulfilling supply contracts rather than an open market, ensuring the developer maintains total control over the player's power curve.

# Part 1: Resource System Design

### 1. Resource Identity
 Resource | Type | Purpose | Thematic Fit |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Titanium Scrap** | Material | Basic building block for ship hulls and station modules. | Salvaged from debris fields. |
 **Ion Cells** | Energy | Used to power jumps and craft high-tech components. | Harvested from gas giants. |
 **Neural Processors** | Tech (Rare) | The primary bottleneck for high-tier upgrades. | Recovered from ancient derelicts. |
 **Galactic Credits** | Currency | Used for assembly fees, repairs, and "bribing" progression gates. | Standardized trade medium. |

### 2. Generation Sources
*   **Titanium Scrap:** High volume, high frequency. Acquired via mining asteroids (10-50 per node). Scales with drill efficiency.
*   **Ion Cells:** Medium volume, daily frequency. Generated by "Siphon Stations" over time. Scales with station level.
*   **Neural Processors:** Low volume, low frequency. Reward for completing "Sector Milestones" or rare salvage missions. Fixed scaling.
*   **Galactic Credits:** Earned by fulfilling "Supply Contracts" (turning in crafted goods). Scales with contract difficulty.

### 3. Consumption Uses
*   **Crafting:** Combining Scrap + Cells + Processors to create Ship Parts (Engines, Shields, Cargo Bays).
*   **Maintenance:** Credits and Ion Cells are consumed to keep the fleet running (daily tax).
*   **Progression Gates:** Large quantities of Credits and Processors are required to unlock new star sectors.

### 4. Storage and Limits
*   **Cargo Hold:** Limits Titanium Scrap and Ion Cells.
*   **Rationale:** Prevents infinite hoarding; forces players to spend or craft to continue gathering.
*   **Overflow:** Excess resources are "vented" (lost) unless the player upgrades storage.

### 5. Exchange Rates (Internal Conversion)
*   **The "Black Market" (NPC):** Players can sell Scrap for Credits at a poor rate (10:1).
*   **Refinery:** Convert 100 Scrap + 10 Cells into 1 "Refined Plate" (used in advanced crafting).
*   *Note: No player-to-player trading exists to prevent RMT and progression skipping.*

### 6. Sink Mechanisms
*   **Fuel Consumption:** Every move between sectors costs Ion Cells.
*   **Repair Costs:** Combat or environmental damage requires Credits and Scrap.
*   **Luxury Tax:** Late-game players pay a "License Fee" (Credits) to maintain their trade empire.

## Balance Report

This document outlines the economic architecture for **Galactic Trader**, a linear strategy game focused on resource management, crafting, and fleet progression.

# Part 1: Resource System Design

### 1. Resource Identity
 Resource | Type | Purpose | Thematic Fit |
 :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
 **Galactic Credits (GC)** | Currency | Standard medium for ship repairs, hiring crew, and base maintenance. | The universal "oil" that keeps the gears of the galaxy turning. |
 **Raw Isotopes** | Material | The primary ingredient for crafting ship components and fuel. | Harvested from asteroids and gas giants; the physical building blocks. |
 **Neural Intel** | Experience | Used to unlock new sectors, tech tiers, and captain skills. | Represents the knowledge and data gathered during exploration. |
 **Void Shards** | Premium | Used for instant crafting, rare cosmetic hulls, and resource boosters. | Mysterious, rare crystals found in the deep reaches of space. |

### 2. Generation Sources
*   **GC:** Earned via mission rewards (60%), selling excess Isotopes to NPC depots (30%), and passive "Trade Route" income (10%). Scales +15% per sector level.
*   **Raw Isotopes:** Acquired through active mining mini-games or automated drones. Yields 50–200 per node. Frequency: High.
*   **Neural Intel:** Gained by completing "First Contact" events, scanning new planets, and successful combat encounters. Fixed amounts per unique action to prevent infinite grinding in low-level zones.
*   **Void Shards:** Daily login rewards (small amounts), achievement milestones, or direct IAP (In-App Purchase).

### 3. Consumption Uses
*   **GC:** Ship repairs (Frequent/Low cost), Crew salaries (Periodic/Medium cost), Purchasing Blueprints (Rare/High cost).
*   **Raw Isotopes:** Crafting "Warp Cells" (Frequent), Upgrading Hull Plating (Medium), Building Station Modules (Rare).
*   **Neural Intel:** Spent at the "Galactic Academy" to unlock the next tier of the tech tree. Once spent, it is gone (Linear progression gate).
*   **Void Shards:** Skipping craft timers, purchasing "Luck Boosters" for mining, or unique ship skins.

### 4. Storage and Limits
*   **Cargo Hold (Isotopes/Credits):** Limited capacity based on ship size. This forces players to return to hubs, creating a gameplay loop of "Sortie -> Harvest -> Return."
*   **Intel/Shards:** No limit. These are "wallet" resources that persist across ship upgrades.
*   **Overflow:** If the Cargo Hold is full, Isotopes are jettisoned (lost). Credits are stored in a "Bank," but only accessible at major ports.

### 5. Exchange Rates (System-Only)
*   **100 Raw Isotopes** → **10 GC** (Selling to NPC).
*   **500 GC** → **50 Raw Isotopes** (Buying from NPC - *Inefficient by design to encourage mining*).
*   **Void Shards** → Can be exchanged for GC or Isotopes in "Emergency Packs," but never for Neural Intel (to protect progression pacing).

### 6. Sink Mechanisms
*   **Maintenance Decay:** High-tier ships require exponentially more GC for upkeep.
*   **Refining Loss:** Crafting advanced components has a 5% "Isotope Waste" factor.
*   **Sector Taxes:** Moving to higher-tier sectors requires a GC "Entry Fee," removing excess currency from the late-game economy.

# Part 2: Comprehensive Balance Report

## 1. Resource Balance
*   **Generation vs. Consumption:** The ratio of Isotope generation to consumption is tuned at **1.4:1**. This ensures players always have a slight surplus for crafting but must manage their cargo space carefully. GC consumption spikes in the mid-game (Tier 3) to prevent "hoarding" before the late-game transition.
*   **Storage Limits:** The "Sortie" limit (Cargo Hold) is the primary friction point. It is balanced to allow 15–20 minutes of gameplay before a mandatory return to base, aligning with mobile/casual session lengths.
*   **Exchange Fairness:** The 10:1 sell/buy spread on Isotopes effectively discourages players from "buying their way" through the material grind using GC, forcing engagement with the mining mechanics.

## 2. Progression Balance
*   **XP (Intel) Curve:** The Intel requirements follow a **quadratic growth curve** ($y = ax^2$). While early levels take 1–2 missions, the final tiers require 15–20 missions. This prevents players from out-leveling the content too quickly.
*   **Unlock Pacing:** New ship classes are unlocked every 5 levels. This provides a "Power Spike" just as the previous tier begins to feel underpowered against scaling enemies.
*   **Skill Tree:** The tree is "Wide but Shallow." Players can specialize in Mining or Combat, but the linear nature of the game ensures they cannot become "stuck" by making a wrong choice.

## 3. Loot Balance
*   **Drop Rates:** Raw Isotopes have a 100% drop rate from nodes, but "Pure Isotopes" (Rare) have a 5% drop rate. This creates a "pity" mechanic where 50 Raw Isotopes can be refined into 1 Pure Isotope, ensuring progress even with bad RNG.
*   **Reward Scaling:** Mission rewards scale linearly with Sector Level. However, ship maintenance scales slightly faster, creating a "Tight Economy" in the end-game that requires optimized play.

## 4. Monetization Balance
*   **Pay-to-Win Risk:** **Low.** Since Neural Intel (Progression) cannot be purchased, whales cannot skip the actual gameplay levels. They can only arrive at the level cap with "shinier" gear and less time spent waiting for timers.
*   **Value Proposition:** Void Shards are most valuable when used for "Permanent Upgrades" (e.g., +10% Cargo Space). This offers better long-term value than "Consumable" skips, encouraging a higher average revenue per user (ARPU).

## 5. Engagement Balance
*   **Daily Commitment:** Optimal play requires ~30 minutes a day (Daily missions + 2 Sorties).
*   **Burnout Prevention:** The "Trade Route" passive income provides GC even when the player is offline, ensuring they don't feel "punished" for taking a day off.
*   **Casual vs. Hardcore:** Casuals can progress through the story using standard gear. Hardcore players can grind for "Overclocked" components which require 5x the resources for a 10% stat increase.

## 6. Overall Recommendations
*   **Critical Issue:** The transition from Sector 2 to Sector 3 shows a potential "Credit Crunch" where ship repair costs may exceed mission earnings for unskilled players.
*   **Suggested Adjustment:** Implement a "Lease-a-Ship" program for players who go bankrupt, allowing them to earn GC at a reduced rate until they can afford their main ship's repairs.
*   **Testing Priority:** Stress-test the "Isotope-to-GC" conversion rate in the late game to ensure players can't bypass the end-game credit sinks by over-farming low-level nodes.
*   **Monitoring Metrics:** 
    *   *Gini Coefficient:* To measure wealth inequality among the player base.
    *   *Resource Velocity:* How quickly are players spending Intel after acquiring it? (Target: < 24 hours).
