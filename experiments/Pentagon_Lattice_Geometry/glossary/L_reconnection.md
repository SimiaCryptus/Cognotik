# L. Reconnection Framework — Detailed Reference

This document expands the **central conceptual framework** of the project: the
classification of polygons and polyhedra by whether their **expansion families**
reconnect into a globally consistent adjacency graph. This is the unifying language
that ties together the algebra, geometry, group theory, and topology of the
multi-sheeted tilings.

---

## The Setup

### Expansion Family

Given a seed polygon (or polyhedron) $P_0$ and a **generator set** $\{g_1, \dots, g_k\}$
of edge-reflections, edge-half-turns, or face-reflections, the **expansion family**
is the set

$\mathcal{F} = \{ g_{i_1} \circ g_{i_2} \circ \cdots \circ g_{i_m}(P_0) : m \geq 0,
\; i_j \in \{1, \dots, k\} \}$

of all tile copies obtained by iterated application. Each generator corresponds to
"crossing" an edge (or face) into a neighboring tile copy.

### Adjacency Graph $\mathcal{G}$

The graph whose vertices are tile copies in $\mathcal{F}$, with an edge between two
tiles if they are related by a single generator (i.e., share an active edge/face).

### The Central Question

> Does $\mathcal{G}$ have many cycles (a "lattice-like" structure), or is it an
> infinite tree?

The presence of cycles is **reconnection**. Their absence is **non-reconnection**.

---

## The Four-Level Hierarchy

The project classifies polygons/polyhedra into **four levels** based on how their
expansion families behave.

### Level 0: Full Reconnection (Flat Tiling)

The expansion family closes into a **single-sheeted Euclidean tiling** of
$\mathbb{R}^n$ — a standard crystallographic tiling.

**Conditions** (all three required):

1. Orientation group $\Gamma$ is finite.
2. All coordinates lie in a single algebraic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.
3. Angular deficit $\delta = 0$ at every vertex.

**Examples**:

- Square (interior angle $90°$, $\delta = 0$).
- Equilateral triangle (interior angle $60°$, $\delta = 0$).
- Regular hexagon (interior angle $120°$, $\delta = 0$).
- Cube, tetrahedron-octahedron honeycomb in 3D.

### Level 1: Multi-Sheeted Full Reconnection

Reconnection holds, but only after passing to a **multi-sheeted covering space**.
The angular deficit is absorbed into **sheet-transition holonomy** at vortex
vertices.

**Conditions**:

1. Orientation group $\Gamma$ is finite.
2. All coordinates lie in a single algebraic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.
3. Angular deficit $\delta \neq 0$, but the lift to the covering space closes.

**Examples**:

- **Regular pentagon** (interior $108°$, deficit $36°$, fiber group
  $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$).
- **Regular dodecahedron** in 3D (fiber group related to binary icosahedral $2I$).
- Other regular $n$-gons with $n$ such that $\pi - 2\pi/n$ is a rational multiple
  of $\pi$.

### Level 2: Edge-Restricted Reconnection

Full reconnection **fails** with all edges/faces active, but reconnection **holds**
when restricted to a chosen subset $E_A$ of edges/faces. These are the **pinwheel
polygons**.

**Conditions**:

1. Orientation group on $E_A$ is finite (even if $\Gamma$ on the full edge set is
   infinite).
2. Coordinates of active edges lie in a single algebraic extension.

**Examples**:

- **Conway–Radin pinwheel triangle** (right triangle, legs $1:2$).
- Various engineered polygons mapping to $\mathbb{Z}^2$ via Gaussian integer
  structure.

The **inactive edges** $E_I = E \setminus E_A$ play the role of decorations or
weak gauge couplings — they do not generate the expansion family but may carry
additional structure.

### Level 3: Non-Reconnective

The expansion family is an **infinite tree** — no two distinct generator words
produce the same tile.

**Failure modes**:

1. **Infinite orientation group**: $\Gamma$ is infinite (irrational rotation angles).
2. **Multiple incompatible irrationalities**: coordinates require two algebraically
   independent extensions (e.g., $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$).
3. **No active subset closes**: even restriction fails.

**Examples**:

- Generic irregular polygons.
- Polygons with irrational angle ratios.
- Most "random" polygons.

## The Reconnection Criteria

These are the **algebraic conditions** for closure of an expansion family.

### Criterion 1: Finite Orientation Group

The **orientation group** $\Gamma \leq O(n)$ is generated by the rotational parts
of all generators (reflections and half-turns squared give rotations).

For closure: $\Gamma$ must be **finite**. Equivalently, all rotational angles
must be **rational multiples of $\pi$** (yielding roots of unity).

**Geometric meaning**: only finitely many distinct tile orientations occur.

### Criterion 2: Single Irrational Base

All vertex coordinates of $P_0$ must lie in a **single quadratic (or higher)
extension** $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Why**: if coordinates require both $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$, then the
expansion family lives in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$, which has degree 4
over $\mathbb{Q}$ and generically produces an infinite lattice point set without
natural closure.

**Examples of allowed bases**:

- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$: square family.
- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$: hexagonal family.
- $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$: pentagonal / icosahedral family.

### Criterion 3 (3D): Dihedral Angle Integrality

In 3D, the analogue of zero angular deficit is **dihedral angle integrality**:
around each edge of the expanded tiling, the dihedral angles must sum to $2\pi$:

$\sum_k \alpha_k = 2\pi.$

For Level 0 (flat reconnection in 3D), this must hold exactly. For Level 1
(multi-sheeted), the deficit is absorbed into sheet holonomy around edges.

## The Pentagonal Pivot: Level 1 in Detail

The **regular pentagon** is the paradigmatic Level 1 case and the project's main
focus. Its multi-sheeted resolution illustrates the framework's full machinery.

### The Angular Deficit

Interior angle: $108° = 3\pi/5$. Sum around a vertex for 3 pentagons:
$3 \times 108° = 324°$. Deficit: $36° = \pi/5$.

### The Fiber Group $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$

The orientation group is $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$ (10 distinct rotation angles, since
$\pi/5$ generates a cyclic group of order 10 under doubling). The natural fiber
group is $G = \mathbb{Z}_{10}$.

### The Multi-Sheeted Cover

The expanded family lives on a **10-sheeted cover** of the punctured plane (or a
related covering space). Each tile is labeled by an element of $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$
indicating its sheet.

### Sheet Transitions $\tau(e)$

Each edge $e$ of the tiling carries a **sheet-transition label** $\tau(e) \in
\mathbb{Z}_{10}$. Crossing $e$ shifts the sheet index by $\tau(e)$.

The **signed3 rule** assigns shifts in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ cyclically around each
vortex, giving a vortex-edge fraction of exactly $2/3$.

### Holonomy and Spinor-Like Behavior

A loop around a vortex accumulates total sheet shift:

$\sum_{e \in \gamma} \tau(e) = \pm 1 \mod 10.$

Two loops: $\pm 2$. Five loops: $\pm 5 = 5 \mod 10$ — and **ten loops** restore
identity.

The structure with $\pm 5$ at "half" the loop count creates the **spinor-like**
behavior: in a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ projection (sheet parity), one loop gives parity
flip ($-1$ in the spinor sense), two loops restore parity.

### The Discrete Principal $G$-Bundle

All this structure assembles into a **discrete principal $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$-bundle**
over the punctured plane $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \Sigma$, where $\Sigma$ is the
set of vortex vertices.

## The Generators in Detail

### Edge Reflections (2D)

For each edge $e$ of $P_0$, the reflection $r_e$ across the line containing $e$ is
an involution: $r_e^2 = \text{id}$. The expanded family treats $P_0$ and $r_e(P_0)$
as adjacent tiles sharing edge $e$.

### Edge Half-Turns (2D)

For each edge $e$, the half-turn $h_e$ about the midpoint of $e$ is an involution
of determinant $+1$. Half-turns preserve orientation; reflections reverse it.
Choice between reflections and half-turns determines whether the tiling has
orientation-reversing symmetry.

### Face Reflections (3D)

For each face $f$ of $P_0$, reflection across the plane of $f$ generates a
neighboring tile. The natural 3D analogue of edge reflections.

### Edge Half-Turns (3D)

For each edge $e$ of $P_0$, the half-turn about the axis along $e$ is an involution
of determinant $+1$. Used in orientation-preserving 3D expansion families.

## Closure and the Cayley Graph Perspective

The expansion family $\mathcal{F}$ is the orbit of $P_0$ under the group

$\langle g_1, \dots, g_k \rangle$

generated by the generators in the affine group $\text{Aff}(\mathbb{R}^n)$.
The adjacency graph $\mathcal{G}$ is essentially the **Cayley graph** of this
group (with the seed $P_0$ as identity), modulo the action on the tile.

- **Level 0–1**: the group is virtually a lattice (crystallographic);
  Cayley graph has polynomial growth.
- **Level 2**: the restricted group is virtually a lattice; full group may not be.
- **Level 3**: the group is virtually free; Cayley graph has exponential growth
  (tree-like).

The transition from polynomial to exponential growth in the Cayley graph **is**
the transition from reconnective to non-reconnective.

---

## Summary: Why the Hierarchy Matters

The four-level hierarchy is the **organizing principle** of the project:

1. **Level 0** connects to classical crystallography and Euclidean tilings.
2. **Level 1** is the new territory: multi-sheeted covers, discrete gauge fields,
   and spinor-like holonomy.
3. **Level 2** connects to pinwheel statistics, extremal geometry, and the
   Erdős/Falconer distance problems.
4. **Level 3** is the "generic" case and shows what's special about the others.

The pentagonal case (Level 1) is the **pivot** between the familiar (Level 0) and
the exotic (Level 3) — and it is where the framework's distinctive phenomena live.
