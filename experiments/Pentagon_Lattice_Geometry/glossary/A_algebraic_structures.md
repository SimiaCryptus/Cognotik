# A. Algebraic Structures and Number Theory — Detailed Reference

This document expands the algebraic and number-theoretic terms used throughout the
multi-sheeted n-gon tiling project. These concepts form the foundation for understanding
why certain polygons reconnect into globally consistent tilings while others do not.

---

## The Algebraic Setting

### Algebraic Field $\mathbb{F}$

The **algebraic field** of a polygon is the smallest field containing all coordinates of
its vertices. This field determines which arithmetic operations are needed to express
all the points generated by repeated reflections and rotations.

- For a square: $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{Q}$ (rational coordinates suffice).
- For a regular hexagon: $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$.
- For a regular pentagon: $\mathbb{F} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ (equivalently $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$).

The **dimension** $[\mathbb{F} : \mathbb{Q}]$ as a vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$ classifies
the expansion family: dimension 1 (rational) gives crystallographic tilings; dimension 2
(quadratic) gives Penrose-like quasiperiodic structures.

### Algebraic Number

An **algebraic number** is a root of a non-zero polynomial with rational (equivalently,
integer) coefficients. Examples:

- $\sqrt{2}$ is algebraic (root of $x^2 - 2$).
- $\phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ is algebraic (root of $x^2 - x - 1$).
- $\pi$ and $e$ are **not** algebraic (they are transcendental).

All vertex coordinates in reconnective tilings must be algebraic — otherwise, the
expansion family cannot close under finite combinatorial rules.

### Cyclotomic Field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$

The **cyclotomic field** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ by a
primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i / n}$. Its dimension over $\mathbb{Q}$
is $\varphi(n)$ (Euler totient).

Cyclotomic fields are the natural home of regular $n$-gons:

- $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ — triangle.
- $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_4) = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ — square.
- $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$ contains $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ — pentagon.

### Cyclotomic Density Trap

A **pathology** occurring when one tries to extend a polygon's tiling by rotations that
are irrational multiples of $\pi$. The orbit of any single vertex under such rotations
becomes **dense** in some circle, ultimately producing a dense subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

This destroys **local finiteness** (only finitely many tiles in any compact region) and
forbids the resulting structure from being a tiling.

The **cut-and-project method** circumvents this trap by restricting projections to those
points falling inside a bounded **acceptance window**.

## Quadratic Fields in Detail

### Quadratic Field

An **algebraic number field of degree 2** over $\mathbb{Q}$, of the form
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ for a square-free integer $d$. Every element has the form
$a + b\sqrt{d}$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}$.

### Real Quadratic Field

A quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ with $d > 0$. The element $\sqrt{d}$ is a real
number, and the field embeds into $\mathbb{R}$. The pentagon's field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$
is real quadratic.

(Imaginary quadratic fields, with $d < 0$, embed into $\mathbb{C} \setminus \mathbb{R}$;
they appear for the equilateral triangle: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$.)

### Quadratic Extension

A field extension $\mathbb{F}(\sqrt{d})$ of degree 2 over a base field $\mathbb{F}$. Often
constructed by adjoining a square root of an element not already in $\mathbb{F}$.

### Discriminant

An invariant capturing the arithmetic of a quadratic field:

- If $d \equiv 1 \pmod 4$: discriminant is $d$.
- Otherwise: discriminant is $4d$.

For $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, $5 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, so the **discriminant is 5**. The
smallness of the discriminant is responsible for the rich arithmetic of $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$
and the unusual properties of pentagonal tilings.

### Ring of Integers

The **maximal order** (largest subring of integers) inside a number field. For
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, the ring of integers is

$$\mathbb{Z}[\phi] = \{a + b\phi : a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$$

(not just $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{5}]$, because $\phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ is also an algebraic
integer).

## The Golden Ratio Universe

### Golden Ratio $\phi$

$$\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180339887$$

Defining properties:

- $\phi^2 = \phi + 1$.
- $\phi^{-1} = \phi - 1$.
- $\phi^n = F_n \phi + F_{n-1}$ where $F_n$ is the Fibonacci sequence.

The golden ratio is the **fundamental unit** of $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$, meaning every unit
(invertible element) has the form $\pm \phi^k$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

### Psi $\psi$

$$\psi = \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{2} = -\frac{1}{\phi} \approx -0.6180$$

The **Galois conjugate** of $\phi$. Together they satisfy:

- $\phi + \psi = 1$
- $\phi \cdot \psi = -1$

Both are roots of $x^2 - x - 1 = 0$.

### Fundamental Unit

The **generator of the infinite cyclic part** of the unit group of an algebraic number
ring. For $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$, the fundamental unit is $\phi$ itself.

**Dirichlet's unit theorem** guarantees a finite collection of fundamental units; for real
quadratic fields, there is exactly one (plus the torsion part $\{\pm 1\}$).

### Unit Group

The **group of invertible elements** of a ring. For $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$:

$$\mathbb{Z}[\phi]^\times = \{\pm \phi^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}$$

These units generate all the special distances appearing in pentagonal lattices.

## Galois Theory

### Galois Automorphism / Conjugation

A **field automorphism fixing $\mathbb{Q}$**. For a quadratic field, there is exactly one
non-trivial automorphism:

$$\sigma : a + b\sqrt{d} \mapsto a - b\sqrt{d}$$

For $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$: $\sigma(\phi) = \psi$. The pair $(\phi, \psi)$ is a Galois
orbit.

### Galois Group

The **group of all Galois automorphisms** of a field extension. For
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})/\mathbb{Q}$, the Galois group is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$,
generated by $\sigma$.

The Galois group encodes the **algebraic symmetry** of the field — and, indirectly, the
geometric symmetry of associated tilings.

## Norm Theory

### Norm Form

The **multiplicative norm** on a quadratic field:

$$N(a + b\sqrt{d}) = (a + b\sqrt{d})(a - b\sqrt{d}) = a^2 - d b^2$$

It maps elements of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ to rationals, and elements of the ring of
integers to integers.

### Norm-Multiplicativity

The key property:

$$N(xy) = N(x) N(y)$$

This means norm factorization is well-behaved. In $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$, two elements of the
same norm can often be factored similarly, leading to **distance-ring coincidences**:
many lattice points share the same distance from the origin.

### Pell Equation

The Diophantine equation

$$x^2 - d y^2 = \pm 1$$

Its integer solutions correspond to **units in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{d}]$**. For $d = 5$:

- $x^2 - 5y^2 = -1$: $(x, y) = (2, 1)$, giving the unit $2 + \sqrt{5} = 1 + 2\phi$.
- Powers of fundamental units generate all solutions.

Pell-equation solutions explain the **rings of unit-distance points** in the pentagonal
lattice.

## Ring-Theoretic Properties

### Principal Ideal Domain (PID)

A commutative ring in which every ideal is generated by a single element.
$\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$ is a PID, which makes its arithmetic essentially as clean as the
integers $\mathbb{Z}$.

### Unique Factorization Domain (UFD)

A ring in which every non-zero non-unit element has a unique factorization into prime
elements (up to order and units). Every PID is a UFD, so $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$ is a UFD.

This **unique factorization** is the reason norm-multiplicativity yields the **pinwheel
phenomenon**: many points share distances because many integers have multiple
factorizations of the same norm.

### Roots of Unity

Solutions of $z^n = 1$. The $n$-th roots of unity form a cyclic group of order $n$.

A rotation of $\mathbb{R}^2$ by angle $\theta$ corresponds to multiplication by
$e^{i\theta}$. The **orientation group** is finite if and only if all generators
correspond to roots of unity — equivalently, if all rotation angles are rational
multiples of $\pi$.

## Number-Theoretic Sequences

### Fibonacci Recurrence

The recurrence

$$F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}, \quad F_0 = 0, F_1 = 1$$

Connection to $\phi$: $F_n = (\phi^n - \psi^n)/\sqrt{5}$ (Binet's formula). The ratio
$F_{n+1}/F_n \to \phi$.

In the pentagonal lattice, **distance rings** appear with spacing controlled by the
Fibonacci recurrence: successive ring radii scale by $\phi^2$.

### Pisano Period

The **period of the Fibonacci sequence modulo $n$**, denoted $\pi(n)$. For example,
$\pi(10) = 60$.

This periodicity controls the **periodicity of $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]/n\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$**
and the orbit structure of lattice points under multiplication by $\phi$.

## Special Algebraic Structures

### Gaussian Integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$

The ring of **complex integers** $a + bi$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$. Norm:
$N(a + bi) = a^2 + b^2$.

$\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is a PID and UFD. It is the natural target lattice for the
**Conway–Radin pinwheel triangle** (1:2 right triangle), since its vertices live in
$\mathbb{Z}^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]$.

### Icosian

A **unit quaternion** of the form $a + bi + cj + dk$ with coefficients in
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ and norm 1. The icosians form the **binary icosahedral group $2I$**
of order 120 — the double cover of the icosahedral rotation group in $SU(2)$.

Icosians provide the algebraic backbone for 3D pentagonal/icosahedral tilings (e.g., AKN
tilings, 600-cell symmetry).

## Why This Algebra Matters

The **degeneracy engine** of pentagonal tilings — the unusually rich coincidence of
distances and directions — traces directly to:

1. **$\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$ is a UFD** (clean prime factorization).
2. **Norm-multiplicativity** (norms factor like rational integers).
3. **Infinite unit group** (Pell solutions generate distance-ring symmetries).
4. **Small discriminant** (many lattice points squeezed into each norm value).

Together these forces conspire so that the Erdős distinct-distance bound is **saturated**
by the pentagonal lattice, and the pinwheel phenomenon emerges.
