# Technical Glossary

A comprehensive reference for terms used across the multi-sheeted n-gon tiling project
(`idea.md`, `affine.md`, `polyhedra.md`, `pinwheels.md`, `erdos.md`, `insights.md`,
`sweep_ngon.md`, `analysis.md`, `experiment.md`, `README.md`). Entries are alphabetized
within thematic sections.

## A. Algebraic Structures and Number Theory

**Algebraic field $\mathbb{F}$** — The smallest field containing the coordinates of a
polygon's vertices. Determines the dimensional class of the expansion family.

**Algebraic number** — A root of a polynomial with rational coefficients. All vertex
coordinates in reconnective tilings are algebraic.

**Cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$** — Field generated by a primitive $n$-th root
of unity. Natural setting for regular $n$-gons.

**Cyclotomic density trap** — Pathology in which orbits of irrational rotations densify
$\mathbb{R}^2$, destroying local finiteness. Avoided by the cut-and-project window.

**Discriminant** — Invariant of a quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$; equals $d$ if
$d \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, else $4d$. For $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, discriminant is 5.

**Fibonacci recurrence** — Linear recurrence $F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}$. Governs
distance-ring spacing in pentagonal lattice (ratio $\phi^2$).

**Fundamental unit** — Generator of the infinite cyclic part of the unit group of an
algebraic number ring. For $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$, the fundamental unit is $\phi$.

**Galois automorphism / conjugation** — Field automorphism fixing $\mathbb{Q}$. For
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$: $\sigma(\sqrt{5}) = -\sqrt{5}$, sending $\phi \mapsto \psi$.

**Galois group** — Group of Galois automorphisms of a field extension. Encodes
algebraic symmetry.

**Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$** — Ring of complex integers $a + bi$, $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Norm $a^2 + b^2$. Underlies pinwheel triangle's $\mathbb{Z}^2$ target.

**Golden ratio $\phi$** — $(1+\sqrt{5})/2 \approx 1.618$. Fundamental unit of $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$;
solves $\phi^2 = \phi + 1$.

**Icosian** — Unit quaternion of the form $a + bi + cj + dk$ with coefficients in
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ and norm 1. Element of the binary icosahedral group.

**Norm form** — Quadratic form $N(a + b\sqrt{d}) = a^2 - db^2$ giving the multiplicative
norm of a quadratic field element.

**Norm-multiplicativity** — Property $N(xy) = N(x)N(y)$. Causes high-multiplicity
distance rings via factorization in $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$.

**Pell equation** — Equation $x^2 - dy^2 = \pm 1$. Solutions in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{d}]$
correspond to units; governs unit distances in pentagonal lattice.

**Pisano period** — Period of Fibonacci sequence modulo $n$. Controls periodicity of
$\mathbb{Z}[\phi]/n\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$.

**Principal ideal domain (PID)** — Ring where every ideal is generated by a single
element. $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$ is a PID, simplifying norm factorization.

**Psi $\psi$** — Galois conjugate of $\phi$: $\psi = (1-\sqrt{5})/2 = -1/\phi \approx -0.618$.

**Quadratic extension** — Field extension $\mathbb{F}(\sqrt{d})$ of degree 2.

**Quadratic field** — Algebraic number field of degree 2 over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Real quadratic field** — Quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ with $d > 0$.

**Ring of integers** — Maximal order of an algebraic number field. For $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$,
it is $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$.

**Roots of unity** — Solutions of $z^n = 1$. The orientation group is finite iff all
rotations correspond to roots of unity.

**Unique factorization domain (UFD)** — Ring with unique prime factorization. $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$
is a UFD; pentagonal norm-multiplicativity follows.

**Unit group** — Group of invertible elements of a ring. For $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$:
$\{\pm \phi^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.

## B. Geometric Constructions

**Active edge / active face** — Edge (2D) or face (3D) carrying a generator operation in
a pinwheel construction.

**Adjacency graph $\mathcal{G}$** — Graph with one vertex per tile, edges between tiles
sharing an active edge/face.

**Affine map** — Map $x \mapsto Ax + b$ combining linear transformation and translation.

**Affine group $\text{Aff}(\mathbb{R}^n)$** — Group of all affine maps of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Ammann-Kramer-Neri (AKN) tiling** — 3D quasiperiodic tiling with icosahedral symmetry;
3D analogue of Penrose tiling.

**Angular deficit** — Difference $2\pi - \sum \theta$ between full rotation and sum of
interior angles around a vertex. Pentagon: $36°$.

**Bitruncated cubic honeycomb** — Space-filling tiling by truncated octahedra; the
Kelvin structure.

**Branched cover** — Cover with ramification points where multiple sheets meet. Pentagon
multi-sheeted cover is branched at vertices.

**Centroid** — Geometric center of a polygon/polyhedron. Used to label tiles.

**Conway–Radin pinwheel tiling** — Aperiodic tiling of $\mathbb{R}^2$ by right triangles
with leg ratio 1:2, exhibiting infinitely many tile orientations.

**Cut-and-project method** — Construction of quasicrystals by projecting a slab of a
higher-dimensional lattice through an acceptance window.

**Dihedral angle** — Angle between two faces of a polyhedron at a shared edge.

**Dodecahedron** — Regular polyhedron with 12 pentagonal faces. Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.

**Duality** — Correspondence between polyhedra exchanging vertices and faces (cube ↔
octahedron; dodecahedron ↔ icosahedron).

**Edge midpoint** — Midpoint of an edge; center of edge half-turns.

**Edge half-turn** — Rotation by $\pi$ about an edge axis; proper isometry of
determinant $+1$.

**Edge-restricted reconnective** — Polygon that fails full reconnection but reconnects
when restricted to a subset of edges.

**Face reflection** — Reflection across a face plane; improper isometry of determinant $-1$.

**Fiber** — Preimage of a point under a projection $\pi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^n$.

**Foliation** — Decomposition of a manifold into parallel leaves (e.g., sheet stack as
foliation of space-like slices).

**Generator** — Group element used to build up the expansion family by repeated application.

**Half-turn** — Rotation by $\pi$.

**Honeycomb** — Space-filling tiling of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

**Icosahedron** — Regular polyhedron with 20 triangular faces. Dual to dodecahedron;
field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.

**Inactive edge / inactive face** — Edge or face carrying no generator (decoration) or
only a weak gauge generator.

**Interior angle** — Angle inside a polygon at a vertex. Pentagon: $108°$.

**Kelvin structure** — Bitruncated cubic honeycomb; conjectured (1887) minimal-surface
partition of space into equal-volume cells.

**Lattice (Bravais)** — Discrete subgroup of $\mathbb{R}^n$ generated by translation.

**Loop** — Closed path in a graph or manifold.

**Monohedral tiling** — Tiling using only one tile shape (up to congruence).

**Octahedron** — Regular polyhedron with 8 triangular faces. Dual to cube.

**Orientation** — Element of the rotation group attached to a tile, recording its angular
state.

**Orientation group $\Gamma$** — Subgroup of $O(n)$ generated by the rotational parts of
all generators.

**Penrose tiling** — Aperiodic tiling of the plane with 5-fold symmetry; cut-and-project
image of $\mathbb{Z}^5$.

**Pentagon** — 5-sided polygon. Regular pentagon: interior angle $108°$, deficit $36°$.

**Platonic solid** — Convex regular polyhedron: tetrahedron, cube, octahedron,
dodecahedron, icosahedron.

**Polygon** — 2D polytope; closed figure bounded by straight line segments.

**Polyhedron** — 3D polytope; solid bounded by flat polygonal faces.

**Polytope** — $n$-dimensional generalization of polygon/polyhedron.

**Quasicrystal** — Aperiodic structure with long-range order and forbidden (e.g., 5-fold)
symmetry.

**Reflection** — Isometry fixing a hyperplane and reversing orientation.

**Reconnection** — Property that an expansion family closes into a globally consistent
adjacency graph rather than diverging as a tree.

**Rhombic dodecahedron** — Polyhedron with 12 rhombic faces; Voronoi cell of the FCC
lattice.

**Sheet** — Copy of $\mathbb{R}^n$ in a multi-sheeted covering space, indexed by a
fiber-group element.

**Sheet index** — Label $s \in G$ identifying which sheet a tile lives on.

**Sheet transition** — Map between sheets associated with an edge/face crossing;
element of the fiber group.

**Tetrahedron** — Regular polyhedron with 4 triangular faces. Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.

**Tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb** — Space-filling tiling combining tetrahedra and
octahedra in ratio 2:1.

**Tiling** — Covering of a space by tiles without gaps or overlaps.

**Triacontahedron** — 30-faced polyhedron dual to the icosidodecahedron; natural
acceptance window for 3D icosahedral quasicrystals.

**Truncated octahedron** — Archimedean solid; only Archimedean space-filler. Tiles
$\mathbb{R}^3$ as the Kelvin honeycomb.

**Vertex** — Corner point of a polygon/polyhedron.

**Voronoi cell** — Region of space closest to a given lattice point.

**Vortex** — Topological defect at a vertex around which sheet transitions accumulate.

## C. Higher-Dimensional Polytopes

**4-cell / 5-cell / 16-cell / 24-cell** — Regular 4D polytopes (4D simplex, hypercube,
cross-polytope, self-dual 24-cell).

**120-cell** — Regular 4D polytope with 120 dodecahedral cells; tiles $S^3$.

**600-cell** — Regular 4D polytope with 600 tetrahedral cells; dual to 120-cell.

**Binary icosahedral group $2I$** — Order-120 double cover of icosahedral group $I$ in
$SU(2)$.

**Hyperbolic tiling** — Tiling of hyperbolic plane; supports otherwise-forbidden
configurations (e.g., 4+ pentagons per vertex).

**Hypercubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^n$** — Integer lattice in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Spherical tiling** — Tiling of a sphere $S^n$. Dodecahedron tiles $S^2$; 120-cell tiles $S^3$.

## D. Group Theory

**Abelian group** — Group with commutative operation.

**Alternating group $A_n$** — Group of even permutations of $n$ elements.

**Binary group** — Double cover in $SU(2)$ of a finite rotation group in $SO(3)$.

**Braid group** — Group of braids on $n$ strands; underlies anyonic statistics.

**Cayley graph** — Graph encoding group structure: vertices = group elements, edges =
generator multiplications.

**Cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_n$** — Group of order $n$ with one generator.

**Dihedral group $D_n$** — Symmetry group of regular $n$-gon, order $2n$.

**Discrete group** — Group with discrete topology; elements are isolated.

**Fiber group $G$** — Group indexing sheets in a covering space.

**Finite group** — Group with finitely many elements.

**Holonomy group** — Group of monodromies around loops in a bundle.

**Icosahedral group $I \cong A_5$** — Rotation group of icosahedron, order 60.

**Klein four-group** — $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, order 4.

**Lift** — Element of a covering group mapping to a given group element.

**Monodromy** — Holonomy of a closed loop in a bundle; element of fiber group.

**Octahedral group $O \cong S_4$** — Rotation group of cube/octahedron, order 24.

**Orthogonal group $O(n)$** — Group of distance-preserving linear maps of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

**$O(n)$ subgroup classification** — In 3D: cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral,
icosahedral, plus reflection extensions.

**Permutation group** — Group acting by permutations of a set.

**Principal $G$-bundle** — Fiber bundle whose fibers are copies of group $G$ with
$G$-action; discrete version models multi-sheeted covers.

**Quaternion group** — Group $\{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$ of order 8.

**Reflection group** — Group generated by reflections.

**Rotation group $SO(n)$** — Subgroup of $O(n)$ preserving orientation.

**Spin group $\text{Spin}(n)$** — Double cover of $SO(n)$.

**$SU(2) \to SO(3)$** — Standard double cover; encodes spinor doubling.

**Symmetric group $S_n$** — Group of all permutations of $n$ elements.

**Tetrahedral group $T \cong A_4$** — Rotation group of tetrahedron, order 12.

**Wallpaper group** — One of 17 crystallographic groups of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

## E. Topology and Bundle Theory

**Acceptance window $W$** — Bounded region in perpendicular space selecting which
ambient-lattice points project to the physical space.

**Branch point** — Ramification point where a covering map has multi-valued behavior.

**Connection** — Rule for parallel transport in a bundle; discrete connection assigns
group elements to edges.

**Covering space** — Map $\pi: \mathcal{M} \to X$ with local homeomorphisms; sheets above
each point.

**CW complex** — Topological space built from cells of increasing dimension.

**Discrete principal $G$-bundle** — Combinatorial analogue of principal $G$-bundle; sheet
group acts on tiles.

**Double cover** — 2-fold covering space. $SU(2) \to SO(3)$ is the canonical example.

**Euler characteristic $\chi$** — Topological invariant $\chi = v - e + f$ for polyhedra;
generalizes alternating sum.

**Fundamental group $\pi_1$** — Group of loop homotopy classes; obstructs simple
connectedness.

**Gauge connection** — Discrete connection in lattice gauge theory; sheet transitions
play this role.

**Holonomy** — Group element accumulated by parallel transport around a loop.

**Local finiteness** — Property that every compact region contains finitely many tiles.

**Manifold** — Locally Euclidean topological space.

**Multi-sheeted cover** — Covering space with multiple sheets; allows projected overlaps.

**Parallel transport** — Transport of fiber elements along paths via connection.

**Perpendicular space $E_\perp$** — Complement of physical space in cut-and-project ambient
lattice.

**Physical space $E_\parallel$** — Subspace of cut-and-project ambient lattice containing
the actual quasicrystal.

**Punctured plane** — $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \Sigma$ for vortex set $\Sigma$. Base of the
pentagon multi-sheeted bundle.

**Quotient space** — Space obtained by identifying equivalent points.

**Riemann surface** — Complex manifold of dimension 1; classical setting for branched
covers like $w = z^{1/n}$.

**Sheet index** — Coordinate in the fiber direction.

**Simply connected** — Having trivial fundamental group; all loops are contractible.

**Topological defect** — Singularity that cannot be removed by local deformation.

**Transition function** — Sheet-change map associated with an edge.

## F. Dimensions and Scaling

**Alexander–Orbach relation** — $d_{\text{spec}} = 2 d_{\text{eff}} / d_w$, connecting
fractal/spectral/walk dimensions.

**Anomalous diffusion** — Diffusion with mean-squared displacement scaling as $t^{2/d_w}$
with $d_w \neq 2$.

**Connectivity dimension** — Hausdorff-like dimension from volume growth $V(r) \sim r^{d_{\text{eff}}}$.

**Dimensional flow** — Scale-dependent dimension; UV value differs from IR value.

**Dimensional reduction** — Decrease of effective dimension at long scales; observed in
CDT and asymptotic safety.

**Effective dimension $d_{\text{eff}}$** — Dimension from volume scaling of BFS balls;
controls geometry.

**Emergent dimension** — Dimension that arises from underlying combinatorial structure
rather than being prescribed.

**Fractal dimension** — Non-integer dimension of self-similar set; $\log N / \log s$ for
$N$ copies at scale $1/s$.

**Hausdorff dimension** — Measure-theoretic dimension defined by covering by small balls.

**IR (infrared)** — Long-distance / long-time regime.

**Power-law growth** — Scaling $V(r) \sim r^d$ defining effective dimension.

**Spectral dimension $d_{\text{spec}}$** — Dimension from return probability scaling
$P_0(t) \sim t^{-d_{\text{spec}}/2}$.

**Sub-diffusion** — Diffusion slower than Brownian; $d_w > 2$.

**Super-diffusion** — Diffusion faster than Brownian; $d_w < 2$.

**Universality class** — Set of systems sharing scaling exponents.

**UV (ultraviolet)** — Short-distance / short-time regime.

**Walk dimension $d_w$** — Exponent in mean-squared displacement $\langle \Delta x^2 \rangle \sim t^{2/d_w}$.

## G. Spectral Graph Theory

**Adjacency matrix $A$** — Matrix $A_{ij} = 1$ if vertices $i,j$ are adjacent.

**Cheeger constant** — Graph isoperimetric ratio; bounds spectral gap.

**Chebyshev polynomial** — Orthogonal polynomial basis used in KPM expansion of density
of states.

**Degree matrix $D$** — Diagonal matrix of vertex degrees.

**Density of states (DOS) $\rho(\lambda)$** — Density of Laplacian eigenvalues; governs
low-energy physics.

**Diameter** — Maximum graph distance between any two vertices.

**Eigenvalue** — Scalar $\lambda$ with $Mv = \lambda v$ for some non-zero $v$.

**Expander graph** — Graph with spectral gap bounded away from 0 as size grows.

**Girth** — Length of shortest cycle.

**Graph Laplacian $\Delta = D - A$** — Discrete analogue of Laplace operator; governs
diffusion.

**Kernel Polynomial Method (KPM)** — Matrix-free spectral approximation via Chebyshev
expansion of DOS.

**Lanczos algorithm** — Iterative method for extreme eigenvalues of sparse matrices.

**Mixing time** — Time for random walk to approach stationary distribution.

**Ramanujan graph** — Graph saturating the Alon–Boppana bound on spectral gap.

**Random walk** — Stochastic process stepping uniformly to neighbors.

**Return probability $P_0(t)$** — Probability that a random walk returns to origin at
time $t$.

**Shift-invert** — Spectral transformation $(M - \sigma)^{-1}$ to access eigenvalues near
$\sigma$.

**Spectral gap** — Smallest non-zero Laplacian eigenvalue $\lambda_1$.

**Spectrum** — Set of eigenvalues.

**Sparse matrix** — Matrix with $O(n)$ non-zero entries; standard for adjacency.

## H. Fractals and Self-Similarity

**Box-counting dimension** — Dimension from counting boxes needed to cover a set.

**Cantor set** — Classical fractal; iterated removal of middle thirds.

**Contraction map** — Map with Lipschitz constant $< 1$; basis of iterated function
systems.

**Fractal** — Self-similar set with non-integer dimension.

**Iterated function system (IFS)** — Finite family of contractions whose attractor is a
fractal.

**Koch snowflake** — Classic fractal curve with dimension $\log 4 / \log 3$.

**Koch tetrahedron** — 3D analogue of Koch snowflake; dimension $\log 6 / \log 3$.

**Menger sponge** — 3D analogue of Sierpiński carpet; dimension $\log 20 / \log 3 \approx 2.727$.

**Self-similarity** — Property of being equal to scaled copies of itself.

**Sierpiński carpet** — 2D fractal; dimension $\log 8 / \log 3$.

**Sierpiński gasket / triangle** — 2D fractal; dimension $\log 3 / \log 2 \approx 1.585$.

**Sierpiński tetrahedron (tetrix)** — 3D Sierpiński; dimension exactly 2.

## I. Cellular Automata

**Birth/survival set** — Pair of integer sets specifying outer-totalistic rule.

**Causal cellular automaton** — CA respecting causal ordering; toy model for discrete
spacetime.

**Causal dynamical triangulation (CDT)** — Quantum gravity approach using causal
triangulated spacetimes.

**Cellular automaton (CA)** — Discrete dynamical system on lattice/graph with local rules.

**Conway's Game of Life** — Canonical 2-state outer-totalistic CA on $\mathbb{Z}^2$.

**Glider** — Stable moving CA pattern.

**Isometric cellular automaton (ICA)** — CA whose update rule depends only on local
graph geometry.

**Lazy graph generation** — On-demand construction of graph vertices/edges as dynamics
expand.

**Outer-totalistic rule** — Rule depending only on a cell's state and the count of live
neighbors.

**Phase transition** — Sharp qualitative change in CA behavior as rule parameters vary.

**Still life** — Stable CA pattern unchanged by update.

**Turing-universal** — Capable of simulating any Turing machine.

**Vortex scattering** — Topology-induced rotation of CA glider trajectories at defects.

## K. Combinatorics and Extremal Geometry

**Adjacency oracle** — Algorithmic procedure returning neighbors of a vertex on demand.

**BFS (breadth-first search)** — Graph traversal by increasing distance from origin.

**Conway–Radin pinwheel** — Aperiodic tiling by right triangles with leg ratio 1:2.

**Delone set** — Discrete set that is both uniformly discrete and relatively dense.

**Distance ring** — Set of points at fixed distance from origin.

**Distance set** — Set of pairwise distances in a point configuration.

**Distance web** — Graph connecting all pairs at a fixed distance.

**Equidistant family** — Set of points mutually at the same distance.

**Erdős distinct distances problem** — Determine minimum number of distinct distances
among $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**Extremal configuration** — Point set achieving extreme value of combinatorial quantity.

**Falconer distance problem** — Hausdorff dimension of distance set of a fractal.

**Guth–Katz theorem** — $\Omega(n / \log n)$ lower bound for distinct distances (2010).

**Incidence geometry** — Study of combinatorial incidences between points and lines/curves.

**Pinwheel polygon** — Polygon engineered so a chosen edge subset reconnects into a
target lattice.

**Spencer–Szemerédi–Trotter** — Bound on unit distances; $O(n^{4/3})$.

**Unit distance problem** — Maximum number of unit-distance pairs among $n$ points.

## L. Reconnection Framework

**Active set $E_A$** — Subset of edges/faces used as generators in pinwheel construction.

**Adjacency rule** — Rule determining when two tiles are adjacent in $\mathcal{G}$.

**Closure (orientation)** — Property that the orientation group is finite.

**Criterion 1** — Finite orientation group (rational-multiple-of-$\pi$ rotations).

**Criterion 2** — Single irrational base (all coordinates in $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$).

**Criterion 3 (3D)** — Dihedral angle integrality: $\sum \alpha_k = 2\pi$ around each
edge.

**Expansion family** — Set of tile copies generated by iterated application of generators
to a seed.

**Four-level hierarchy** — Classification: full reconnection / multi-sheeted full /
edge-restricted / non-reconnective.

**Generator set** — Set of operations (reflections, half-turns) used to expand the family.

**Inactive set $E_I$** — Edges/faces not used as generators (decoration or sheet
transitions).

**Multi-sheeted full reconnection** — Level 1: full reconnection but angular deficit
forces multi-sheeted cover.

**Non-reconnective** — Level 3: expansion produces an infinite tree.

**Reconnection criteria** — Algebraic conditions for closure of an expansion family.

**Reconnective polygon** — Polygon whose expansion family reconnects.

**Restricted expansion family $\mathcal{F}_A$** — Family generated by active subset only.

**Translation vector $\mathbf{b}_k$** — Translation part of an affine generator.

**Tree (non-reconnective)** — Acyclic infinite graph produced by failed reconnection.

## O. Project-Specific Terminology

**Degeneracy engine** — Mechanism by which $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ algebraic structure
forces many distance coincidences.

**Pinwheel phenomenon** — Density of distance-direction pairs in pentagonal lattice.

**signed3 rule** — Default vortex rule assigning sheet shifts in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$
cyclically; vortex edge fraction exactly $2/3$.

**Spinor-like holonomy** — Discrete analogue of spinor double cover: single loop returns
$-1$, double loop restores identity.

**Vortex edge fraction** — Fraction of edges carrying non-zero sheet shift; $2/3$ under
signed3.

**Vortex rule** — Rule assigning sheet transitions to edges.
