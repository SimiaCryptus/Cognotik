# D. Group Theory — Detailed Reference

This document expands the group-theoretic vocabulary used throughout the multi-sheeted
n-gon tiling project. Group theory governs the symmetries of tiles, the structure of
fiber groups indexing sheets, and the algebraic obstructions to flat-space tiling.

---

## Basic Group-Theoretic Notions

### Abelian Group

A group $(G, \cdot)$ with **commutative** operation: $ab = ba$ for all $a, b \in G$.
Examples: $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}_n$, $\mathbb{R}$ under addition.

Non-abelian examples relevant to the project: symmetric groups $S_n$ for $n \geq 3$,
quaternion group, dihedral groups $D_n$ for $n \geq 3$, all rotation groups in 3D except
$SO(2)$.

### Discrete Group

A group with the **discrete topology**: all elements are isolated. Examples:
$\mathbb{Z}$, the icosahedral group $I$, the modular group $PSL_2(\mathbb{Z})$.

### Finite Group

A group with **finitely many elements**. Classification of finite simple groups is one
of the great achievements of 20th-century mathematics. Finite groups appear as
**orientation groups**, **fiber groups**, and **rotation groups of Platonic solids**.

---

## Common Finite Groups

### Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_n$ (also $C_n$)

The group of order $n$ generated by **one element**: $\mathbb{Z}_n = \langle g \mid g^n = 1 \rangle$.
Realized as **integers modulo $n$** under addition, or as **$n$-th roots of unity** under
multiplication.

In the project: $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$ is the orientation group of the pentagon expansion
family (10 rotational copies of the tile).

### Dihedral Group $D_n$

The **symmetry group of the regular $n$-gon**, of order $2n$. Generated by a rotation
$r$ of order $n$ and a reflection $s$ of order 2 with relation $srs = r^{-1}$.

Presentation: $D_n = \langle r, s \mid r^n = s^2 = (rs)^2 = 1 \rangle$.

### Klein Four-Group

The group $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, **order 4**, abelian, every non-identity
element has order 2. Smallest non-cyclic group.

### Symmetric Group $S_n$

The group of **all permutations** of $n$ elements. Order $n!$. Non-abelian for $n \geq 3$.

### Alternating Group $A_n$

The group of **even permutations** of $n$ elements. Subgroup of $S_n$ of index 2, hence
order $n!/2$. Simple for $n \geq 5$.

Importance: $A_5 \cong I$ (icosahedral rotation group).

### Quaternion Group

The group $Q_8 = \{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$ of **order 8**. Defined by:

$$i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$$

Non-abelian, smallest non-trivial double cover. Foundational for spin physics and the
binary icosahedral group.

## Rotation and Symmetry Groups

### Orthogonal Group $O(n)$

The group of **distance-preserving linear maps** of $\mathbb{R}^n$, equivalently $n \times n$
matrices with $A^T A = I$. Has two components: determinant $+1$ (rotations) and $-1$
(reflections).

### Rotation Group $SO(n)$

The **special orthogonal group** — orientation-preserving subgroup of $O(n)$, consisting
of matrices with **determinant $+1$**. The identity component of $O(n)$.

### $O(n)$ Subgroup Classification in 3D

The finite subgroups of $SO(3)$ are classified:

- **Cyclic** $C_n$: rotation by $2\pi/n$ about an axis.
- **Dihedral** $D_n$: $C_n$ together with $n$ perpendicular half-turns.
- **Tetrahedral** $T \cong A_4$: order 12.
- **Octahedral** $O \cong S_4$: order 24.
- **Icosahedral** $I \cong A_5$: order 60.

Plus **reflection extensions** in $O(3)$: adding inversions or reflections.

This classification limits which orientation groups can occur in flat-space tilings.

### Tetrahedral Group $T$

The **rotation group of the regular tetrahedron**, isomorphic to $A_4$, order 12.
Generated by 8 face rotations and 3 edge rotations (plus identity).

### Octahedral Group $O$

The **rotation group of the cube/octahedron**, isomorphic to $S_4$, order 24.

### Icosahedral Group $I$

The **rotation group of the icosahedron/dodecahedron**, isomorphic to $A_5$, order 60.
A simple group — one of the smallest non-abelian simple groups. Plays the same role for
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$-tilings that $S_4$ plays for $\mathbb{Z}$-tilings.

## Spin Groups and Double Covers

### Spin Group $\text{Spin}(n)$

The **double cover of $SO(n)$**. Algebraically, $\text{Spin}(n)$ has the same Lie algebra
as $SO(n)$ but a "twisted" topology where the loop $2\pi$-rotation generator becomes
**non-trivial** (it takes a $4\pi$ rotation to return to identity in $\text{Spin}(n)$).

### $SU(2) \to SO(3)$

The **canonical double cover** in 3D. $SU(2)$ is the group of $2 \times 2$ unitary
matrices with determinant 1, isomorphic to the unit quaternions. The map sends each
unit quaternion $q$ to the rotation $v \mapsto q v \bar{q}$.

Encodes **spinor doubling**: spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ wave functions live in representations
of $SU(2)$ that are not representations of $SO(3)$.

### Binary Group

The **double cover in $SU(2)$** of a finite rotation group in $SO(3)$. For each finite
subgroup $H \subset SO(3)$, the binary group $\tilde H = \pi^{-1}(H)$ in $SU(2)$ has
order $2|H|$.

Examples:

- Binary tetrahedral $2T$, order 24.
- Binary octahedral $2O$, order 48.
- Binary icosahedral $2I$, order 120.

### Binary Icosahedral Group $2I$

**Order-120 double cover** of $I$ in $SU(2)$. Equivalently, the **120 icosian
quaternions**. Equivalently, the vertex set of the 600-cell.

Importance: encodes the **spinor-like holonomy** of pentagonal multi-sheeted covers.

### Lift

An **element of a covering group mapping to a given group element**. In a double cover
$\tilde G \to G$, each $g \in G$ has exactly two lifts $\pm \tilde g \in \tilde G$.

## Reflection Groups and Beyond

### Reflection Group

A group generated by **reflections** in hyperplanes. Includes:

- Finite reflection groups (Coxeter groups of finite type).
- Symmetry groups of Platonic solids (with reflections included).
- Wallpaper and crystallographic groups.

### Wallpaper Group

One of the **17 crystallographic groups of $\mathbb{R}^2$** — the 17 inequivalent symmetry
groups of periodic planar patterns. These are precisely the groups containing two
linearly independent translations. The pentagon is excluded from this list: no
wallpaper group has 5-fold rotational symmetry.

### Permutation Group

A group acting by **permutations of a set**. Every group is a permutation group (Cayley's
theorem), and every finite group embeds into some $S_n$.

## Bundle and Connection Group Theory

### Fiber Group $G$

The **group indexing sheets** in a covering space or principal $G$-bundle. Tiles on
different sheets are related by $G$-action. For pentagons in the project:
$G = \mathbb{Z}_{10}$ (cyclic of order 10, since the deficit $36° = 360°/10$ is the
smallest rotation absorbing pentagonal frustration).

### Principal $G$-Bundle

A **fiber bundle whose fibers are copies of group $G$** equipped with $G$-action by right
multiplication. Local trivializations:

$$\pi^{-1}(U) \cong U \times G$$

with transition functions valued in $G$. **Discrete principal $G$-bundles** model the
multi-sheeted covers in the project: each edge crossing yields a $G$-element (the
sheet transition).

### Cayley Graph

A **graph encoding the group structure**:

- **Vertices**: group elements.
- **Edges**: between $g$ and $gs$ for each generator $s$ in a chosen generating set.

Properties:

- $G$ acts on its Cayley graph by left multiplication (graph automorphisms).
- Different generating sets give different Cayley graphs (but with the same underlying
  group).
- Useful for **visualizing finite groups** and computing geodesic word lengths.

### Holonomy Group

The **group of monodromies around loops** in a bundle. For a discrete principal
$G$-bundle, the holonomy group is a subgroup of $G$ generated by all loop monodromies.

### Monodromy

The **holonomy of a single closed loop** — an element of the fiber group obtained by
accumulating sheet transitions along the loop. For the pentagon's multi-sheeted cover,
the monodromy of a vortex-encircling loop equals the deficit angle, $36°$.

## Braid Theory

### Braid Group

The **group of braids on $n$ strands**, denoted $B_n$. Generated by $n-1$ elementary
braids $\sigma_i$ (swap of adjacent strands), with relations:

$$\sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \quad (|i - j| \geq 2)$$
$$\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$$

The second is the **braid relation**, central to the Yang–Baxter equation.

Importance:

- Underlies **anyonic statistics** in 2D quantum systems.
- Quotient by $\sigma_i^2 = 1$ gives the symmetric group $S_n$ (bosons/fermions).
- Mapping class group of the $n$-punctured disk equals $B_n$.

## Why Group Theory Drives the Framework

The reconnection criteria translate directly into group-theoretic conditions:

1. **Finite orientation group $\Gamma$**: requires rotational angles to be rational
   multiples of $\pi$ (Criterion 1). Limits $\Gamma$ to be a subgroup of $C_n$ or $D_n$
   (in 2D), or one of the finite subgroups of $SO(3)$ classified above (in 3D).

2. **Discrete fiber group $G$**: encodes which sheets exist. Typically cyclic
   ($\mathbb{Z}_n$) for 2D pentagonal cases, but may be richer (binary icosahedral) in
   3D.

3. **Sheet transitions** are $G$-valued **discrete connections** on the adjacency graph;
   their holonomy around vortex loops generates a subgroup of $G$.

4. **Spinor-like behavior** arises when the fiber group is a double cover (e.g., $2I$),
   reproducing in discrete combinatorial form the same algebraic phenomenon that gives
   spinors their $4\pi$-rotation symmetry.

Group theory is thus not just background — it provides the precise language in which
both **obstructions** (finite group requirements) and **resolutions** (sheet transition
groups) are expressed.
