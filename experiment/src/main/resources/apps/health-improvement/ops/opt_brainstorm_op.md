* Review the patient's responses to the latest follow-up questions alongside the prior brainstorm and perspectives.
* Brainstorm **new or revised** candidate diagnoses that were not previously considered, or that become more plausible
  given the additional information.
* Re-evaluate previously dismissed or low-ranked possibilities — does the new data resurrect any of them?

## New Symptom Patterns

- Identify any newly revealed symptom clusters, timeline details, or contextual factors
- Note how these patterns differ from or extend the original presentation

## Expanded Differential Candidates

- Propose additional conditions organized by body system, including those that only become apparent with the new data
- Consider atypical presentations of common conditions and typical presentations of uncommon conditions
- Revisit conditions previously ruled out — do the patient's answers change their plausibility?

## Red Flag Re-Check

- **IMMEDIATE ACTION:** Re-screen for any newly disclosed red-flag symptoms (e.g., weight loss, night sweats, new
  neurological signs, worsening trajectory)
- Flag any urgent considerations that emerged from the patient's responses

## Information Gaps

- Note which questions the patient was unable to answer or answered ambiguously
- Identify how remaining uncertainty affects the breadth of the differential
- Suggest what categories of information would most effectively narrow the list
