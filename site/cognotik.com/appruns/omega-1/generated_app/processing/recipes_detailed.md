## Breakfast: Miso-Ginger Soft-Boiled Eggs with Toasted Nori Toast

**Astrological Alignment:** Scorpio Sun's transformative intensity meets Pisces Moon's gentle nourishment. Miso (fermented, deep umami) honors Scorpio's depth; soft-cooked eggs provide easily digestible protein for sustained morning energy.

### Ingredients (1 serving)

- 2 large eggs
- 1 tbsp white miso paste
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, minced
- 1 sheet nori (seaweed), cut into strips
- 1 slice good quality bread (sourdough preferred)
- 1 tsp butter or olive oil
- ½ cup water
- Pinch sea salt
- Fresh cilantro (from windowsill garden), chopped

### Instructions

1. Bring ½ cup water to boil in small saucepan
2. Gently lower eggs into boiling water using spoon
3. Cook for 6-7 minutes (soft yolk, set whites)
4. While eggs cook, toast bread until golden
5. Dissolve miso paste in 2 tbsp warm water, add minced ginger
6. Brush toast lightly with butter or oil
7. Arrange nori strips on toast
8. Remove eggs with slotted spoon, place in egg cup or small bowl
9. Drizzle miso-ginger mixture over eggs
10. Serve toast alongside, garnish with cilantro

**Prep Time:** 5 minutes | **Cook Time:** 8 minutes | **Total:** 13 minutes  
**Yield:** 1 serving

**Nutritional Information (per serving):**
- Calories: 285
- Protein: 14g
- Carbohydrates: 22g
- Fat: 12g
- Fiber: 2g
- Iron: 3.2mg (18% DV)
- B12: 0.6mcg (10% DV)
- Magnesium: 35mg

**Astrological Notes:**
- **Elements:** Water (miso fermentation, nori seaweed), Fire (ginger warmth)
- **Symbolism:** Eggs represent potential and transformation (Scorpio rebirth); miso's fermentation mirrors Scorpio's alchemical nature; nori connects to ocean depths (Pisces Moon)
- **Optimal Timing:** Early morning (8:30 AM) aligns with Sagittarius Rising's dawn energy; ginger activates digestive fire before illustration work
- **Supports Goals:** B12 for mental clarity; easily digestible protein prevents energy crashes; fermented miso supports hormone balance

**Cost Estimate:** $2.10/serving (eggs $0.60, miso $0.40, nori $0.50, bread $0.40, ginger $0.20)

**Cooking Tips:**
- Use a timer for precise egg cooking—6 minutes yields runny yolk, 7 minutes yields jammy center
- Quality bread makes a difference; sourdough's fermentation adds digestive benefits
- Miso should not boil (destroys beneficial bacteria); add to warm water only
- Cilantro from windowsill garden reduces cost and adds fresh brightness

**Substitutions:**
- If nori unavailable: use thinly sliced cucumber or avocado on toast
- If miso unavailable: use 1 tbsp tahini mixed with ginger and lemon juice
- Bread: any whole grain option works; focus on quality over white bread

## Lunch: Seared Mackerel with Roasted Root Vegetables & Fermented Daikon

**Astrological Alignment:** Mackerel (omega-3 rich, deep ocean fish) embodies Scorpio's intensity and Pisces Moon's water connection. Roasted root vegetables ground the meal in earth energy despite water dominance. Fermented daikon adds Scorpio's transformative fermentation theme.

### Ingredients (1 serving)

**For the fish:**
- 5 oz mackerel fillet (skin-on preferred)
- 1 tsp olive oil
- ¼ tsp sea salt
- Pinch black pepper
- ½ lemon, cut into wedge

**For roasted vegetables:**
- 1 cup mixed root vegetables (carrots, parsnip, beets), cut into ¾-inch cubes
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- ¼ tsp sea salt
- Pinch black pepper
- ½ tsp dried thyme

**For fermented daikon:**
- ⅓ cup daikon radish, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- ½ tsp sea salt
- ½ tsp sugar
- 1 small dried chili (optional, for fire element)

### Instructions

**Prepare fermented daikon (can be made ahead):**
1. Combine daikon slices with salt in small bowl
2. Let sit 5 minutes to release moisture
3. Mix rice vinegar and sugar together
4. Pour over daikon, add dried chili if using
5. Let sit at room temperature 30 minutes minimum (or refrigerate up to 3 days)

**Roast vegetables:**
6. Preheat oven to 425°F
7. Toss root vegetables with olive oil, salt, pepper, and thyme
8. Spread on sheet pan in single layer
9. Roast for 20-25 minutes, stirring halfway through, until caramelized and tender

**Sear fish:**
10. While vegetables roast, pat mackerel dry with paper towel
11. Heat 1 tsp olive oil in cast iron skillet over medium-high heat
12. Place mackerel skin-side down in hot skillet
13. Sear 4-5 minutes without moving (skin should crisp)
14. Flip carefully, cook 2-3 minutes until flesh is opaque
15. Season with salt and pepper, squeeze lemon over top

**Plate:**
16. Arrange roasted vegetables on plate
17. Place seared mackerel alongside
18. Top with fermented daikon and its liquid
19. Garnish with fresh cilantro or parsley

**Prep Time:** 10 minutes (plus 30 min fermentation) | **Cook Time:** 25 minutes | **Total:** 35 minutes  
**Yield:** 1 serving

**Nutritional Information (per serving):**
- Calories: 420
- Protein: 32g
- Carbohydrates: 28g
- Fat: 18g
- Fiber: 5g
- Omega-3 (EPA+DHA): 2.1g
- Iron: 2.8mg (16% DV)
- Magnesium: 48mg (12% DV)

**Astrological Notes:**
- **Elements:** Water (mackerel, fermented daikon), Earth (root vegetables), Fire (black pepper, optional chili)
- **Symbolism:** Mackerel's deep ocean origin connects to Pisces Moon's watery intuition; fermentation represents Scorpio's transformative power; roasted vegetables ground the water-dominant chart
- **Optimal Timing:** Lunch (1:00 PM) provides sustained energy for afternoon illustration work; omega-3s support mental clarity during creative sessions
- **Supports Goals:** Omega-3s for sustained energy and mental clarity; fermented daikon aids digestion; iron from fish and vegetables supports energy; easily digestible preparation

**Cost Estimate:** $6.80/serving (mackerel $3.50, root vegetables $1.20, daikon $0.80, oil/seasonings $0.50)

**Cooking Tips:**
- Mackerel's skin crisps beautifully in cast iron; don't move it while searing
- Root vegetables caramelize better when cut uniformly and not crowded on pan
- Fermented daikon can be made in batches (3-day shelf life) for multiple meals
- Lemon juice brightens the rich mackerel flavor and aids iron absorption

**Substitutions:**
- Mackerel: sardines, wild salmon, or halibut (adjust cooking time based on thickness)

## Snack (3:30 PM): Dark Chocolate & Pumpkin Seed Energy Bites

**Astrological Alignment:** Dark chocolate (Scorpio's depth and intensity) combined with pumpkin seeds (earth grounding, hormone-balancing phytoestrogens) addresses the 3:30 PM creative energy dip while supporting seed cycling goals.

### Ingredients (makes 8 bites; 2 per serving)

- ⅓ cup raw pumpkin seeds
- ⅓ cup unsweetened cocoa powder
- ¼ cup raw honey or maple syrup
- 2 tbsp coconut oil
- 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
- ¼ tsp sea salt
- ¼ tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch cayenne pepper (optional, for fire element activation)

### Instructions

1. Toast pumpkin seeds in dry skillet over medium heat for 3-4 minutes, stirring frequently
2. Let cool slightly, then pulse in food processor until roughly chopped (not powder)
3. Add cocoa powder, salt, and cayenne to processor
4. Pulse to combine
5. Warm honey and coconut oil together (can microwave 30 seconds)
6. Add honey mixture and vanilla to processor
7. Pulse until mixture holds together when pressed
8. Line small plate or baking sheet with parchment paper
9. Roll mixture into 8 balls (about 1 tbsp each)
10. Refrigerate at least 30 minutes before serving
11. Store in airtight container in refrigerator up to 2 weeks

**Prep Time:** 15 minutes | **Chill Time:** 30 minutes | **Total:** 45 minutes  
**Yield:** 8 bites (2 per serving)

## Dinner: Steamed Halibut with Miso Broth & Bok Choy

**Astrological Alignment:** Steaming (water element cooking method) honors Pisces Moon's gentle nature. Miso broth represents Scorpio's fermented depth. Bok choy provides earth grounding and easily digestible greens.

### Ingredients (1 serving)

**For the broth:**
- 1 cup dashi stock (or vegetable broth)
- 1 tbsp white miso paste
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, minced
- ½ tsp soy sauce
- 1 green onion (white part only, cooked—respects raw onion intolerance), sliced

**For the fish:**
- 5 oz halibut fillet
- 1 tbsp sake or dry white wine
- ¼ tsp sea salt

**For vegetables:**
- 1.5 cups bok choy, cut into 2-inch pieces
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- Pinch sea salt

**Garnish:**
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
- Fresh cilantro or shiso (from windowsill garden)
- Thin lemon slice

### Instructions

**Prepare broth:**
1. Heat dashi stock in small saucepan over medium heat
2. Add minced ginger and soy sauce
3. Simmer 3 minutes to infuse flavors
4. Dissolve miso paste in 2 tbsp warm broth (don't boil)
5. Stir miso mixture back into broth
6. Add sliced green onion (white part only)
7. Keep warm on low heat

**Steam fish and vegetables:**
8. Fill steamer pot with 2 inches water, bring to boil
9. Pat halibut dry, season with salt and sake
10. Place halibut on steamer rack
11. Arrange bok choy around fish
12. Cover and steam 8-10 minutes until fish is opaque and bok choy is tender-crisp
13. Toss bok choy with sesame oil

**Plate:**
14. Pour miso broth into shallow bowl
15. Carefully place steamed halibut in center
16. Arrange bok choy around fish
17. Sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds
18. Garnish with cilantro or shiso and lemon slice

**Prep Time:** 10 minutes | **Cook Time:** 12 minutes | **Total:** 22 minutes  
**Yield:** 1 serving

**Nutritional Information (per serving):**
- Calories: 310
- Protein: 38g
- Carbohydrates: 12g
- Fat: 10g
- Fiber: 2g
- Omega-3 (EPA+DHA): 0.8g

- **Supports Goals:** Omega-3s for mental clarity; B12 for energy; fermented miso aids digestion; gentle cooking method respects sensitive digestive system

**Cost Estimate:** $5.20/serving (halibut $3.00, dashi/broth $0.80, miso $0.40, bok choy $0.60, sesame $0.40)

**Cooking Tips:**

## Breakfast: Tempeh Scramble with Sautéed Mushrooms & Kimchi

**Astrological Alignment:** Tempeh (fermented soy, complete protein) and kimchi (fermented vegetables, Scorpio's transformative theme) create a breakfast that honors both digestive health and Scorpio's depth. Mushrooms add umami richness.

### Ingredients (1 serving)

**For tempeh:**
- 3 oz tempeh block
- 1 tsp olive oil
- ¼ tsp sea salt
- Pinch black pepper
- ½ tsp soy sauce

**For mushrooms:**
- 1 cup mixed mushrooms (cremini, shiitake), sliced
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- ¼ tsp sea salt
- Pinch black pepper

**For assembly:**
- ⅓ cup kimchi (store-bought or homemade)
- 1 green onion (white part only, cooked), sliced
- 1 tbsp fresh cilantro
- 1 slice whole grain toast

### Instructions

1. Steam tempeh block for 10 minutes to soften (reduces bitterness)
2. Let cool slightly, then crumble into bite-sized pieces
3. Heat 1 tsp olive oil in cast iron skillet over medium-high heat
4. Add crumbled tempeh, cook 5-7 minutes until golden, stirring occasionally
5. Season with salt, pepper, and soy sauce
6. Transfer tempeh to plate, keep warm
7. In same skillet, heat 1 tsp olive oil over medium heat
8. Add sliced mushrooms, cook 4-5 minutes until they release moisture
9. Add minced garlic, cook 1 minute until fragrant
10. Season with salt and pepper
11. Toast bread while cooking
12. Combine tempeh and mushrooms on plate
13. Top with kimchi and its liquid
14. Garnish with green onion and cilantro
15. Serve with toast on the side

**Astrological Alignment:** Poaching (gentle water-element cooking method) honors Pisces Moon's intuitive, flowing nature. Avocado (creamy, nourishing) provides healthy fats for sustained energy. Microgreens add nutrient density and fresh brightness.

### Ingredients (1 serving)

- 2 cups water
- 1 tbsp white vinegar
- Pinch sea salt

**For toast:**
- 1 slice good quality bread (sourdough preferred)

# COOKING TIPS & TECHNIQUES

## Fish Preparation

### Searing Fish
1. Pat fish dry with paper towel (moisture prevents browning)
2. Heat oil in cast iron skillet until shimmering
3. Place fish skin-side down in hot skillet
4. Don't move fish for 4-5 minutes (allows skin to crisp)
5. Flip carefully and cook 2-3 minutes on other side
6. Fish is done when opaque and flakes easily

### Steaming Fish
1. Fill steamer pot with 2 inches water, bring to boil
2. Pat fish dry and season lightly
3. Place on steamer rack
4. Cover and steam 8-10 minutes until opaque
5. Fish is done when it flakes easily with fork

### Poaching Fish
1. Bring broth or water to gentle simmer (not boiling)
2. Place fish in simmering liquid
3. Cook 8-10 minutes until opaque
4. Don't let liquid boil vigorously or fish will break apart

### Baking Fish
1. Preheat oven to 400°F
2. Pat fish dry and place on parchment-lined sheet pan
3. Brush with oil and season
4. Bake 12-15 minutes until opaque
5. Fish is done when it flakes easily

## Vegetable Preparation

### Roasting Vegetables
1. Cut vegetables uniformly (¾-inch cubes or wedges)
2. Toss with olive oil, salt, pepper, and herbs
3. Spread on sheet pan in single layer (don't crowd)
4. Roast at 400-425°F for 20-25 minutes
5. Stir halfway through for even browning
6. Vegetables are done when caramelized and tender

### Steaming Vegetables
1. Fill steamer pot with 2 inches water, bring to boil
2. Cut vegetables into bite-sized pieces
3. Place on steamer rack
4. Cover and steam 8-10 minutes until tender-crisp
5. Don't overcook or vegetables become mushy

### Sautéing Vegetables
1. Heat oil in skillet over medium-high heat
2. Add vegetables in batches (don't crowd pan)
3. Cook 4-5 minutes until tender-crisp
4. Season with salt, pepper, and herbs
5. Don't overcook or vegetables become mushy

## Grain Preparation

### Cooking Rice
1. Rinse rice under cold water until water runs clear
2. Use 1:2 ratio of rice to water
3. Bring to boil, then reduce heat to low
4. Cover and simmer 18-20 minutes until water is absorbed
5. Let rest 5 minutes before fluffing with fork

### Cooking Farro or Quinoa
1. Rinse grain under cold water
2. Use 1:3 ratio of grain to water
3. Bring to boil, then reduce heat to low
4. Cover and simmer 20-25 minutes until tender
5. Let rest 5 minutes before fluffing with fork

### Cooking Lentils
1. Rinse lentils under cold water
2. Use 1:3 ratio of lentils to water
3. Bring to boil, then reduce heat to low
4. Simmer 20-25 minutes until tender but not mushy
5. Drain excess water if needed

## Fermented Food Preparation

### Making Miso Soup
1. Heat dashi stock to gentle simmer
2. Add vegetables and simmer 3-5 minutes
3. Remove from heat
4. Dissolve miso paste in 2 tbsp warm broth
5. Stir miso mixture into soup (don't boil)
6. Serve immediately

### Making Pickled Vegetables
1. Slice vegetables thinly
2. Place in small bowl with salt
3. Let sit 5 minutes to release moisture
4. Mix vinegar and sugar together
5. Pour over vegetables
6. Let sit 30 minutes minimum (or refrigerate up to 3 days)

### Making Fermented Vegetables (Sauerkraut/Kimchi)
1. Shred vegetables finely
2. Mix with salt (2% by weight)
3. Pack into clean jar, pressing down to release liquid
4. Liquid should cover vegetables
5. Cover with cloth or fermentation lid
6. Keep at room temperature 3-7 days
7. Taste daily until desired fermentation level
8. Transfer to refrigerator to slow fermentation

## Sauce & Dressing Preparation

### Making Tahini Dressing
1. Whisk together tahini, lemon juice, minced garlic
2. Add warm water gradually to reach desired consistency
3. Season with salt and pepper
4. Adjust lemon juice or water as needed

### Making Miso Glaze
1. Whisk together miso paste, mirin, soy sauce
2. Add minced ginger and sesame oil
3. Brush on fish before baking
4. Glaze will caramelize in oven

### Making Aioli
1. Combine mayonnaise, minced garlic, lemon juice
2. Season with salt and pepper
3. Whisk until well combined
4. Use immediately or refrigerate up to 3 days
